The Big Ocean

Forge on the 2026-11-07 brainstorm (PR #326) · Hullcracker.io · session 2026-11-07 · 19 memlog entries, 12 locks, 1 kill

Outcome

“A marine radar simulator that happens to have a naval battle royale attached.”

The slice is hardened and smaller than it came in. The ocean does not grow. The reach does. Realism is kept as ratios (radar ≈ guns, optics shorter, the arc wider than the pattern, reach about the size of the board), never as kilometers. Time compression is now a ruling instead of an accident. The set is a real marine radar with five real knobs and a wide WWII beam. Every open ruling Friday handed over is closed except one dial parked with the night arm.

Locked

  1. Clear-day dark is resolution, not range. Radar and guns reach about equally far. A contact is an arc, sharp in range and wide in bearing, wider than a salvo pattern at gun reach. No fire-control set; splashes sharpen the arc.
  2. No AIM row. Five index rows. The dispersion curve is the dial, never a row.
  3. Dispersion is an ellipse growing with range, ~1 % along the shot and a third of that across, flat damage, same function for secondaries, pure and seeded. Point blank is a knife fight.
  4. Time compression is explicit. One factor on every rate; “knots” is a label; the clock stays 16 minutes.
  5. The board stays 10,000 u. One scale factor maps real distances onto it: radar ~9,000 u, guns ~8,700 u, hull by eye ~4,600 u, class by eye ~3,000 u. Ring, cap and reveal beat stand; today's tuning already sits at the playable limit.
  6. Beacons are charted at the midpoint between two neighboring hulls, nudged inward by at most 30 s of sailing. Replaces a rule that pushed them away from players.
  7. Gain's cost is server-made ghosts. Indistinguishable for one sweep; a ship persists, a ghost does not. Every knob that shapes the paint is a server-side input.
  8. The set: modern chart-overlay display, WWII antenna. Knobs: gain, sea clutter, rain clutter, pulse length, range scale. Beam width is a CONFIG dial, not a control.
  9. A splash is a one-sweep radar return for anyone sweeping it. Shells in flight stay sight-only.
  10. Weather is a return-strength model on two axes. Rain first; clear, night/clear and night/rain are test arms.
  11. Zoom out to the whole board. Phone is not a target.

Killed and rejected

  • MARPA. Real on modern sets; hands the player course and speed, which is the thing “lead in your head” forbids. Out, like fire control.
  • The 40–50 km board. At 30 u/s a ring step plus a full center shift cannot be sailed inside the two-minute reveal window; the storm would kill edge players before any shot. The only ways to keep it were an earlier reveal and a half-step cap, and then the whole cycle is a chase.
  • True knots at a real scale. A 30-knot hull cannot reach the center of a 44 km board inside a 16-minute match. The alternatives (today's 10 km board, or an hour-long match) kill the slice or the browser game.
  • AIM index row; beam-width and sector-scan as player knobs; a kilometer-fixed beacon band; the fleet-anchor beacon rule.

Weak points that survived

  • Clear day may be a turkey shoot: a 20 km gun on a 22 km pond at 0:00. The dial is the ghost floor at zero gain. Untested.
  • Ghost rates and the “ghost near a real ship” rule are unmeasured; the honest player's reading load is unknown.
  • Equidistant beacons favor the faster hull; the neighbor-ambush meta is accepted as the game.
  • A beacon can still end up in the storm after the real ring shifts. The nudge is not a safety promise.
  • A 9,000 u set with a range-scaled cell is lumpy up close; zoom-level lattices are unbuilt.
  • Bots see ghosts too, and need a hit-probability hold fraction that depends on the dispersion function.
