- (note) PHYSICS ANSWER (Eric's question): torpedo 60 u/s ~= 54kt, a 2kt current is a 3.6% cross-track set - real but the smallest error in the problem, AND modern torpedoes are guided (gyro/inertial/wire/terminal homing) so they correct continuously. Moored mines are ANCHORED and do not drift - current makes them DIP, not travel; bottom mines never move; drifting mines are prohibited under Hague VIII as indiscriminate. A minefield that drifts away is not a minefield. Ships are the one thing currents unambiguously affect: set and drift is THE classic current effect. FR47 was ALREADY a pure movement mechanic - Eric's prior design was the physically sound one and the report pulled the feature toward the claim physics supports least
- (crack) THE STRUCTURAL WEAKNESS, and it is the honest answer to 'is it fun': CURRENT IS SYMMETRIC. Islands, fog, radar shadows and sightlines all generate asymmetry - they hide you from him differently than him from you. A current perturbs both combatants in the same water equally, which is a reference-frame change and close to no perturbation at all. Current only becomes tactical at GRADIENTS, where one ship is in fast water and the other is not. So the gameplay lives at the BOUNDARIES of fast water, not inside it - which argues for sharp-edged eddy lines rather than smooth fBm gradients, and argues that an ocean-wide ambient field is the wrong shape for the feature
- (crack) DECISION FREQUENCY is the other honest measure. A mechanic's value is roughly how often it changes what you do times how much. Ambient current: 4-6%, constant, symmetric, changes nothing = near zero. Strait current: maybe twice a match, moderate magnitude. Whirlpool: while you are in it, EVERY SECOND, high magnitude - heading rotating, position drifting, aim solution moving. The fun is concentrated in discrete visitable places, not in a field

QUINN'S REFORMULATION: symmetry is not a property of the FORCE, it is a property of the force PLUS the players' freedom to leave.
