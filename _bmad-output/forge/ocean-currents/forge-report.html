Ocean Currents

Pressure-tested against Realistic Ocean Currents in Hullcracker (technical research, 2026-08-11) and against the shipped codebase.

The idea held up. The build is deferred.

Not killed — every load-bearing question got an answer, and the answers are written down so a future pickup doesn't re-derive them. What stopped it was neither cost nor feasibility.

"Trivial to add, but depending on how it works it could change the game in ways that range from virtually imperceptible all the way to major effects on game balance."

Revisit when players ask for it

The turn of the session

The facilitator's verdict was that currents are symmetric — a force acting equally on both combatants in the same water is a reference-frame change, not a mechanic — and that the ocean-wide field was therefore near-worthless. The bench took that apart.

🔬 Dr. Quinn · Master Problem Solver
"Symmetry isn't a property of the force. It's a property of the force plus the players' freedom to leave. Two ships drifting in the same water are on a treadmill — but only while both of them can choose to step off it."

The storm removes that choice. Current becomes asymmetric precisely inside the shrinking ring — and because the ring is offset-center and server-rolled, which water the finals are fought in differs every match. Cloud drew the corollary immediately: this kills the fBm field harder rather than rescuing it. You need current in a few places the ring can find, not everywhere.

Ruled

  • ✓Currents are worth having — judged on fun, with a high skill ceiling explicitly accepted. The novelty is real: no shipping naval game has them.
  • ✓One system. A current field supersedes FR47 whirlpools rather than coexisting; whirlpools survive only as features inside the current dynamics. Takes the planned shared/src/sim/whirlpool.ts and the server-secret hemisphere with it unless rehomed.
  • ✓Cursor = launch bearing; draw the resulting curve; never auto-solve it. Keeps the skillshot, rejects the torpedo-data-computer.
  • ✓Representation must be on the water and deducible — a battleship or mine layer needs a channel that isn't a weapon.

Proposed, not ruled

  • ?Movement mechanic, not shooting. The question was asked and answered; the answer was never ratified — and Samus attacked the procedure, not the conclusion.
  • ?Place-based, ambient ocean still. Forced by movement-only: a uniform 4–6% set is a feature nobody feels.
  • ?Skip the fBm stream function entirely. Placed primitives get the gameplay at a fraction of the machinery. You can tune a strait; you cannot tune fBm.
  • ?Current × storm is the actual feature. Quinn's reframe, above.

Rejected, with reasons

  • ✕Public field with a deliberately hidden aim curve. That rule binds only honest players — a modified client integrates the public field and draws it. It would be the first mechanic in the game to hand a cheat client real mechanical advantage, against a standard held absolutely every cycle.
  • ✕Drifting mines as the default. Moored mines are anchored and dip; they don't travel. Drifting mines are Hague VIII prohibited as indiscriminate. Survives only as an opt-in MineMode doctrine.
  • ✕The research report's deflection table as the centerpiece. Its self-declared "single most important number" is the claim the physics supports least — a guided torpedo corrects, and 2 kt against 54 kt is a 3.6% set.
  • ✕"Ship the seam dormant." The height-raster precedent doesn't transfer: inert storage nobody reads is not live code in the prediction path.

Weak points that survived scrutiny

  • △Samus's procedural strike, unanswered. Eric ruled semi-realism over realism in the radar round — killed the mast-height card, rejected per-seed H. Movement-only was then decided on "true" rather than "fun," by the criterion he'd already overruled once. The pivot may still be right; it was reached the wrong way.
  • △Legibility is marginal at honest strength. Every wake-bow instrument lands at 3–5%: 11u of bow across a 247.5u track, 5.5u across a torpedo's 165u. Readable in a strait, nearly invisible in open water.
  • △Movement-only concentrates all engineering risk on hull drift through prediction — the one integration the report flagged. Mitigated, not removed, by shipping a steady field.
  • △Divergence-free means no convergence lines. Foam and debris — the most legible real-world current tell — is unavailable by construction. Lost the moment the method was chosen.
  • △Any representation needs a static form, because motion=off is honored across the attention-tier table. A current shown only through animation fails the exact player it most needs to reach.

Verified against the code, not taken on trust

  • ✓stepShell: 19 references in server/src, zero in client/src — ordnance drift genuinely carries no prediction risk.
  • ✓stepShip calls Math.cos/Math.sin every tick — the zero-transcendental rule is a generation-path rule, not a runtime one. Written down nowhere else in the project.
  • ✓The torpedo never reads input.aimDist — it is already a pure bearing weapon clamped to the bow ±30°. The gun is the point weapon, and a shell flies through air.
  • ✓Mines already move, and the legality clamp is shipped: creepMines plus clampMinePoint — never outside the disk, never ashore, "mines float."
  • ✓Cost was never the constraint: 2.2 µs/tick, 64 KB/room, against radar shadows at 278 µs and radar wakes at 425–470 µs.

If it comes out of the pocket

One placed vortex, one placed strait jet, hull drift only — no field, no bake, no noise — plus a zoneOverride to force the endgame ring onto them. Half a day. The kill criterion is honest and easy to check: after twenty minutes of sailing, do you ever think about the water when you're not inside one of the two features?
