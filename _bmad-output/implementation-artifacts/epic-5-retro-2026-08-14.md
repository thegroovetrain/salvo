## The shape of this epic, in one paragraph

Epic 5 was planned as six stories about making the ocean alive and dying legible. It delivered **four of them in four days** — the fastest epic the project has run — and then stopped, by owner ruling, one story short of the water features it is named for. What actually happened is that the death beat turned out to be **three separate design conversations stacked on one five-second window**: what the sim does (5-1/5-2), what the player *sees* (amendments 18 → 21 → 32, three consecutive rulings on the same beat), and what the screen afterwards says (5-3). Every one of those was corrected at least once after it shipped, and every correction came from Eric sailing the build. Meanwhile 5-6 quietly became the largest single story in the project's history — a fleet retool, a roster deletion, a fill deletion and a 36% bigger ocean in one cycle — and needed a same-day balance reversal because its damage numbers were wrong in a way no test could see. The epic ends with the systems layer declared **complete enough**, and the work in front of it is no longer mechanics.

## What Went Well

1. **The spec contract held for a fifth consecutive epic: 0 intent_gap, 0 bad_spec** — through a story that carried five features at once, three reversals of shipped presentation, and a mid-epic scope ruling that ended the epic two stories early. Five epics is no longer a streak; it is evidence the question-gate-then-spec shape works.

2. **The perception boundary held at SIX for a second consecutive epic, and it held against real pressure.** Three separate features this epic wanted a new channel and each found a narrower home instead: the sinking deadline rides a **self-private `OwnShip` key** (amendment 16); the aggro bracket rides a **self-private per-contact flag** (40); and `SunkEvent.vcls` rides the **existing `sunk` row, gated strictly narrower than the row itself** (43). Amendment 19 is the epic's best single piece of boundary reasoning — it *found a leak in its own story's AC*, proved the window is derivable by composing two already-public channels, and accepted it on the Bounty precedent rather than papering over the AC's claim.

3. **Correct-on-sight remains the project's best mechanism, and this epic is its cleanest demonstration.** Amendment 20 is the exemplar. The orchestrator ruled the match should not be held open for a sinking hull, reasoning carefully about a twenty-player lobby — *"the last kill is ONE death out of 19."* Eric opened two tabs, ran a 1v1, and reported: *"No sinking window, the game just immediately ends."* **In a duel every death is the match-ending death**, so the truncation was not an edge case; it was 100% of duels and 100% of how one person tests the game. The recorded lesson — **a feature invisible in the most common way it is exercised is not shipped** — generalizes past this beat.

4. **The `latch the outcome, defer only the transition` fix is better architecture than the thing it replaced.** Rather than inventing a `finishing` sub-state, `checkWin` resolves and records the winner at the instant it first sees ≤1 afloat captain and never re-derives it. That makes amendment 14 (*"sinking does not affect the outcome"*) true **by construction rather than by timing** — the sink order can grow during the hold and the result cannot move. Plus a safety net at `latch + window + 1000 ms`, commented as a net rather than a mechanism, because a match that can never finish is catastrophic.

5. **Eric's economic derivation on the PvE fleet is the most valuable single ruling of the epic** (amendment 45). The headline was *"oops, they are too strong"*; the actual finding was far worse and entirely his: at 6/8/10 damage, **the PvE farm was XP-NEGATIVE.** Two small hulls cost ~48 hp to clear and paid ½ level, while 50 hp of repair costs a full banked level — so a captain who farmed *correctly and won* came out behind one who ignored the fleet. The feature's whole premise ("my XP has a second faucet") was inverted by its own damage numbers, and **no amount of skill fixed it, because the loss was in the exchange rate rather than the execution.**

6. **Deletion stayed a first-class ruling, and got bolder.** The match-start drone fill was removed *outright* — the fill, `dronesSmoke.mjs` in full, the test suite's fill cases, the batch-sim harness's fill and its `--drones` flag — with keeping it behind `HC_DEV_OPTIONS` explicitly offered and declined. Amendment 41 also names its own cost in the same breath (AR18's committed tuning method loses its implementation) rather than discovering it later. Same house style as Epic 4's grey NO-DATA and storm wall.

7. **A long-parked contingency was un-parked and closed with evidence.** Sudden death — carried as an Eric-gated parked item since Epic 3's retro and explicitly *never to be built absent a new ruling* — got its ruling and shipped in one cycle, superseding epic-3 amendment 24 on its post-closure-shrink clause **only** (the damage ramp stayed unauthorized, `stormDps` untouched). The pacifist control, previously `unresolved` **by structure**, now resolves **12/12 at p50 16:27**. The evidence document also corrects its own earlier draft's ceiling claim rather than quietly shipping it.

8. **`STEP_ORDER` landed as data with an identity test, and the hazard was named before it bit.** Amendment 6 is precise about why: at least four steps are correct *only because of where they sit*, most sharply `tickRepairs` — *"damage wins the tie by construction, with no explicit tie-break code"* — so a one-line reorder would let regen un-sink a hull at 0 hp. A declared `before:`/`after:` constraint engine was considered and rejected as more machinery than a 16-element array warrants. The existing rationale comments moved onto their rows.

9. **`ShipRecord.alive` was replaced rather than shadowed**, across 44 non-test sites, with no compatibility getter — on the explicit reasoning that a derived boolean shadow is two representations of one truth, *the exact desync class `effectiveStats()` exists to prevent*. The cost was named up front as the largest part of the diff and paid rather than mitigated.

## Challenges & Growth Areas

1. **Three of the epic's five orchestrator rulings were overturned, and each was defensible in the abstract.** Amendment 8 (survivor placement tiers) → superseded by amendment 9 the same day (*"ffs just stop counting drones"* — it found a defensible ORDER for rows that should not have existed). Amendment 17 (don't hold the match open) → **vetoed by Eric on first contact with the water.** Amendment 18's plume clause → reversed by amendment 32 on the argument that a plume is an *event mark*, not a *look*. Epic 4's lesson was *"rulings made before seeing the thing have an error rate."* Epic 5 sharpens it: **the error is concentrated in orchestrator rulings about presentation, and it is concentrated in the ones that reason from the general case toward the specific.** All three were caught within a day, which is the system working — but each cost a cycle.

2. **Three consecutive cycles shipped with no live or visual verification, on entirely presentational work.** The 5-3 ledger entry says so in plain text and names exactly what wanted an eye: whether whole-map framing reads as *a reveal* or just *very far away* (hull scale drops to ~0.26×, and the mockup's own legend flags that all-hull nameplates may read as CLUTTER at that zoom); whether the 0.62 dim leaves the reveal legible without hurting modal contrast; whether the `motion: off` snap reads as broken rather than instant. 5-6 is in the same state — fleet spacing, whether the aggro bracket reads at the edge of the bubble, whether a 2800 u ocean *feels* emptier. **The honesty is exemplary and the gap is real.**

3. **No multiplayer playtest for a third consecutive epic** — and this epic added mechanics that are *specifically* multiplayer-shaped. The 5-second sinking window's whole design (you can take your killer with you) has never been exercised by two humans who want to. The bounty throne, the public register, the aggro bracket under contest, and Epic 3's carried terminal-ring checkpoint all still sit on single-observer or zero-observer evidence.

4. **Story 5-6 carried five features and its own frontmatter said so** (`warnings: [multiple-goals]`). Fleet retool + roster removal + fill deletion + aggro UX + a 36% bigger ocean, in one cycle, generating 12 amendments and needing a same-day rebalance. It landed cleanly, which is a real achievement — but the same-day reversal of its central balance table (amendment 45) is what a story that big looks like when it goes *well*. Nothing modelled that cost in advance.

5. **Rebase renumbering churn is now a measurable tax.** One commit reads *"Story 5-6: renumber ~180 amendment citations around cycle 81's amendment 32."* Cycles 83/84 collided, then 85/86/87 collided again; the tagline cycle was authored as cycle 85 / amendment 45 and **renumbered twice.** Parallel worktree cycles against an append-only, numerically-cited amendment ledger will keep doing this. Nobody has proposed a fix and it is not obviously worth one — but it is real friction and it is now the third epic where it appears.

6. **The deferred-work ledger grew 178 → 220 while resolving two.** No triage pass since 2026-07-18, now the second epic that commitment has gone unmet. Individually every defer is evidenced; collectively the ledger is an archive with a queue's name.

7. **A tracker drifted despite the standing rule.** `spec-5-6-roving-pve-fleets.md` still carries `status: 'ready-for-dev'` in its frontmatter while `sprint-status.yaml` records the story as done. Small, but this is exactly the class of thing the both-trackers-every-PR rule exists to catch, and it survived a review gate.

8. **A beta-facing regression shipped by ruling, and its premise was false.** Amendment 46 (c) retired `LAST HULL FLOATING WINS` from the home page on the stated premise that HOW TO PLAY carries the rule — **it does not**; that control paints the `FIELD MANUAL ARRIVES IN A LATER REFIT` stub. The win condition is now stated **nowhere a new player can read it**; the only surviving statement is the results banner, shown solely to the player who already won. The ruling stands as given and the implementation is faithful — but it was taken on a false premise, it was caught by a review model rather than by the person who ruled it, and **it is a first-run-experience defect in the exact epic where the owner declared beta readiness the priority.**

9. **Sudden death guarantees a DEATH but not a WINNER, and no tiebreak exists.** From 16:00 every afloat hull takes identical damage, so two hulls entering the collapse with equal HP sink on the **same tick** — which amendment 14 defines as a DRAW. Two untouched same-class captains are equal by construction, and that is precisely the stand-off the feature exists to break. Uniform synchronized damage makes same-tick mutual destruction *structurally more likely here than on any combat path*. Related: once the ring is at r=0 the outcome is fully determined by remaining HP and banked heals, so a battleship that avoided every fight beats a torpedo boat that won three. Neither is a defect under Eric's *"geometry, not the damage curve"* constraint, and **no tiebreak should be invented** — but it is an open design ruling sitting on the match's terminal state.

## Key Insights

1. **A feature invisible in the most common way it is exercised is not shipped** (amendment 20). The reasoning that produced the defect was *correct arithmetic about the wrong population*. Any ruling that weighs "how often does this bite?" must ask **how often does it bite the way this game is actually played and tested right now** — which today is one person with two tabs.

2. **Presentation splits by the QUESTION a channel answers, not by the beat it lands on.** Amendment 18 moved identity and location apart correctly, then moved the plume with the *location* half — and amendment 32 corrected it because **a plume is an event mark, not a look.** A 0.9 s expanding ring says *a hit landed here*, and the event it marks is the holing. A persistent tint says *this thing is a wreck*, and that is only true at founder. One beat, two channels, two different answers. The same shape governs amendment 38's feed/record split: **transient feedback and the persistent tally are different questions** — you must know each sinking landed; the tally is what stays empty.

3. **The exchange rate is the balance number, not the DPS** (amendment 45). A reward system's health is `what you gain ÷ what it costs you to undo the damage you took getting it`. 50 hp of repair costs one banked level, so any PvE damage profile must be checked against what its victims *pay to undo it*. Amendment 33's original balance note computed time-to-clear and damage-per-volley and never computed this — which is how a feature shipped whose entire premise was numerically inverted.

4. **A ruling's *criterion* outlives its *cases*.** Amendment 10's sinking-activation policy is stated as **fitment, not category** — *"it is in a ship equipment slot so it meets criteria for usability"* — so a future equipment row is admitted by default rather than needing a new ruling. Compare amendment 45's waves, which Eric explicitly reframed from numbers into a **ratio** (one fleet per ~5 captains, held constant as the storm thins the field): *"the level totals are the consequence of that ratio, not the input to it."* Rulings written as criteria and ratios do not need re-litigating; rulings written as tables do.

5. **The obvious fix for a constraint violation is usually the one that destroys the constraint.** Amendment 37: constraining only the fleet *anchor* let hulls scatter to 260 u from a captain — inside the sight bubble, a visible pop-in. Inflating the anchor's denied radius was costed and rejected because at a full roster it would exceed the whole map, so **every wave would take the fallback and the rule would stop meaning anything.** The fix was to constrain **per hull**: the formation deforms, the wave never fails. Same shape as amendment 122's dazzle clamp in Epic 4 — the intuitive widening re-breaks the property it was protecting.

6. **A reviewer will infer a rule from the existence of a field, and be confidently wrong.** Amendment 35 records that the cross-model gate flagged the global witness sweep as a CONFIRMED defect, reasoning from `fleetId` that fleets should be separate aggro networks. **That rule was never made** — the gate is line of sight and nothing else; `fleetId` shares a waypoint stream and carries no combat meaning. The amendment says *"do not 'fix' this"* in as many words, because *the next reviewer will find it too.* **A ledger entry that pre-refutes a plausible false finding is cheaper than the third review cycle that rediscovers it.**

7. **Two representations of one truth is the recurring architectural defect class, and the project now reflexively refuses it.** `ShipRecord.alive` was deleted rather than shadowed (amendment 2). The bounty throne is server-owned even though the client could derive it. And amendment 38 immediately flagged its own consequence — with `kills` no longer counting drones, `kills` and `captainKills` are **identical by construction**, so Story 4.6's split is now redundant and *retiring it is the honest simplification, but it touches a ratified split and must be a deliberate reviewed edit, not a silent collapse.*

8. **Telemetry and presentation are different questions, and the split has now been re-derived three times.** Amendment 9 settled it (*"the operator's data, not presentation"*), amendment 38 accidentally violated it by making PvE kills leave no trace anywhere, and amendment 44 restored it with `pveKillsByClass`. Worth promoting to a standing rule: **when a presentation ruling empties a counter, check whether telemetry was reading that counter.**

## 🚨 Significant Discovery — Epic 6 inherits a capability Epic 5 deleted

**Story 6.4 (Combat-Bot AI) has an acceptance criterion that no longer has an implementation behind it.**

6.4's AC reads: *"bot quality is measured, not felt: the batch-sim harness's third duty — bot-vs-bot evaluation scored on kill distributions, match lengths, storm deaths (AR12)."*

Amendment 41 deleted the match-start drone fill **and everything built on it**, explicitly including `batchsim/runner.ts`'s fill and its `--drones N` flag. The amendment names the cost and homes it correctly (*"Epic 6's combat bots are where that capability would be rebuilt"*), and a `deferred-work.md` entry exists. But the consequence for planning is concrete and is not yet in any epic's scope line:

1. **6.4 cannot measure bot quality on day one** — it must first rebuild a synthetic-lobby harness that Epic 5 removed. That is real work inside a story already carrying utility scoring, a staggered observe cadence, and a lint-enforced import boundary.
2. **AR12's other two duties went with it** — the pre-launch load test (`loadTest.mjs`, which Story 7.6's release gate depends on by name) and economy tuning. Amendment 44's `pveKillsByClass` covers the economy leg from live matches, which is *better* evidence than the harness produced. **The load-test leg is uncovered and it is a beta gate.**
3. **The new fleet AI is not the bot AI.** `game/fleetAI` (self-defence, witness propagation, waypoint streams) shares no boundary with 6.4's perception-gated combat bots — which may only read `perception.observe()`. Nothing here is reusable as bots, and the ledger should not let that assumption form.

**Secondary, and squarely on the beta path:** **Story 5-1's drone-gate ruling handed Story 6-5 a debt, and 6-5 is now beta-critical.** With the win check counting captains only, drones can never win *and* a human can no longer lose to them by attrition — so a solo-human-versus-drones match has **no defined end**. Production is safe today only because `CONFIG.match.minHumans` is 2. That same constant means **a single player cannot start a match at all**, which for a beta at population zero is the central problem, and Solo vs AI (6-5) is the ratified answer to it. So the story that fixes the beta's biggest structural gap is also the story carrying an undefined termination rule created two epics earlier.

**Third:** **amendment 42 hard-coded `baseRadius` 2800 and re-ratified the closing-rate band** (worst-case escape 0.799 → 1.019, replacing the pinned 0.75–0.85 band). Story 6.2 owns roster-dynamic sizing and must now **replace a literal with a curve and re-derive the band as a function of radius** — the amendment says so. It also flagged that `heightField.ts`'s `regionWavelength: 2400` is a fixed world-unit literal that was sized to span the disc exactly once and now repeats across a 2800 u map. **That wants an eyeball pass and has not had one.**

## Next Epic Preparation — and the beta reframe

Eric's ruling is not only a deferral; it is a **change of phase**: *"i have enough systems."* Epic 5 closes the mechanics arc. What follows is read against one question — **what stands between the shipped build and a beta a stranger can play?**

Epic 6 (Honest Lobbies & Modes) is the next epic as planned, and it holds the answer, but **its internal order matters more than it used to.** The observations that should shape it:

**The population problem is structural, not polish.** `CONFIG.match.minHumans = 2` means one player cannot start a match. At beta launch, population is zero. The ratified answer is **Solo vs AI (6-5), backed by combat bots (6-4) and reachable through mode select (6-6)** — this is the *"there is always a real game"* spine, and it is the beta's critical path. 6-1 (queue lobbies) is what makes Standard honest; 6-6's queue-liveness steering is the dead-queue mitigation D6 already names.

**Three stories on that path carry inherited debt created this epic:**

- **6-5** owes the solo-termination rule that 5-1 created (see the alert above).
- **6-4** must rebuild the batch-sim capability amendment 41 deleted before it can satisfy its own measurement AC.
- **6-2** must replace amendment 42's hard-coded 2800 with a curve and re-derive the closing-rate band as a function of radius — and re-check `regionWavelength`.

**6-3 is mostly already done.** Amendment 4 dropped the drone gate in 5-1, and amendment 9 made results captain-only. What remains is formal multi-mode coverage and the tests the AC names — the smallest story in the epic now.

**Epic 7 carries two items that have quietly become urgent rather than final-polish:**

- **7-4 (How-to-Play)** now also owns the win-condition gap amendment 46 opened. A beta where the objective is stated nowhere is a first-run defect, not a documentation nicety.
- **7-1 (NFR1 whole-frame verdict)** now carries **two full epics of unmeasured render growth** — Epic 4's radar instrument plus Epic 5's bigger ocean, 63 fleet hulls, and a whole-map reveal framing that draws every coastline and contour band at once. This is the largest unquantified risk in the project, and it is the one item where discovering the answer late is genuinely expensive.

**And one thing that is not an epic at all:** the single highest-value action available right now is **a multiplayer playtest**, because it is the only instrument that discharges a three-epic backlog of eye-dependent claims in one sitting — and because a beta's first honest signal is other people playing.
