## Goal

Make the game "a marine radar simulator that happens to have a naval battle royale attached." The board stays 10,000 u; the "big ocean" is real reach on it. Radar, guns, eyes and torpedoes reach by real ratio to the board (one scale factor, about 0.5), and the set behaves like a real marine radar: contacts paint as arcs, the operator tunes the set and pays for gain with ghost returns, splashes paint, and rain weakens returns. Realism of the set decides knob questions, the goal is fun, and ratios are kept, not kilometers. Epic 10 finishes first on today's ocean. Epic 11 is its own production release: every story is its own PR and build cycle on `development`, and nothing reaches production until the whole ocean has had its staging pass.

## Requirements & Constraints

- Every number is Eric's, ruled at the story's spec step via AskUserQuestion. Never fill one in; mark unruled clauses `[unruled]`. Forge figures are `[dial]`s for the bench page and his harness, not specs.
- Shell damage is flat anywhere in a dispersion pattern. Distance decay is retired. Point blank is a no-miss knife fight, accepted.
- A contact is an arc: sharp in range, wide in bearing (about 5-6 degrees, a `CONFIG` dial, not a player knob), wider than a salvo pattern at gun reach. There is no fire-control set and no MARPA. Clear-day dark is resolution, not range.
- Player knobs (gain, sea clutter, rain clutter, pulse length, range scale) are free and instant. INTEL stays sweep speed. The index has five rows (ARMOR, POWER, RELOAD, ENGINES, INTEL); RANGE and AIM are gone, so reach is a weapon fact in hectometers.
- Gain buys server-made ghost returns. A ghost never repeats in place and a ship does. Ghosts sometimes land near real ships. The client draws nothing it wasn't sent.
- A splash is a one-sweep return. Shells in flight stay sight-only and never paint on radar.
- Weather is fixed per match. The first build is rain (day) only. Clear, night/clear and night/rain are test arms, and which arms ship is Eric's call on staging.
- Time compression is one named factor on every rate. Naming it must not move any number.
- Untouched: map generation, height raster, islands, the zone ring ladder, `offsetCap`, the reveal beat, the 16 min clock, spawns, hull size and polygons. No friendly fire.
- The rung-ratio tests die on purpose when the twelfths ladder retires. Record that as a ruling in the amendments, never patch them to pass.
- Performance claims are measured on staging, never on a Vite dev build or Eric's MacBook. Quick balance runs are capped at 99 matches and never launched unprompted.
- No new in-game copy unasked. Explanations go to How-to-Play in Eric's copy (11.11). A design gap found at review is an AskUserQuestion before the PR, never a ledger entry.

## Technical Decisions

- Dispersion is one pure shared function (aim point, range, seed) to landing point: an ellipse growing linearly with range (about 1 % along, about 1/3 of that across, plus a point-blank floor), seeded from the match stream. Main battery, secondaries and every shell-type weapon use it. Range-attenuation code and `CONFIG.gunAttenuation` are removed with dated notes.
- Reaches become named hectometer facts in `CONFIG`. Sight has two tiers (class-ID silhouette, full hull) through `perception.observe()`. The twelfths `RUNG` ladder retires (built, Story 11.2 — amendment 4).
- The arc extends `paintCoverage` / `paintMask` (server-side, so the wire still carries only a cell mask). It is not a new channel. The client radar cell scales with range, and zoom lattices are built only if a staging capture demands them.
- Five knob fields ride `InputMsg` through `inputs.ts` (finite-checked, clamped, silently dropped if malformed). They are stored per observer in `World` and applied in `observe()` only. A return-strength model (range, target size, later rain along the path) decides what paints. Nothing below the observer's gain ever leaves the server.
- Ghosts and splashes are new `SIGNAL_REGISTRY` sources through `observe`, each with its own invariant line (a ghost carries no real position; a splash reveals only landing position to the arc's precision). The perception invariant keeps exactly SIX exceptions, extended and never widened.
- Weather rolls once at room creation, rides the welcome beside `fogSeed`, and is not on the schema. The fog density field is the rain.
- Beacons are charted at the midpoint between neighboring hull pairs, then nudged toward the ring center until inside the hypothetical next ring, by at most 30 s of baseline sailing (a dial). Min spacing, the captain keep-out disc and counts 8/4/2 stand. `pickFleetAnchor` stays for spawns only.
- `PROTOCOL_VERSION` bumps when the client reads new data (reach block, knob intents, splash event, welcome weather). Otherwise it does not.
- Bots stay functional in every story and sail one honest knob setting. They may not import `world.js`. 11.9 is the tuning story: a hit-probability hold fraction from the dispersion function and a persistence read.

## UX & Interaction Patterns

- Free camera: pan and zoom anywhere, the whole board as minimum zoom, one key snaps home. Zoom is the range scale. Markers (own hull, held contacts, beacons, mines) hold screen size at every zoom. Each salvo leaves a fading peg on the chart (the firer's own knowledge). No scope, no first-person. Phone is not a target. Camera tests use moving ships.
- The knob HUD, the marker size rule, peg look, rain look and night flash size are rendered candidates shown to Eric before commit. Read DESIGN.md first.
