## The shape of this epic, in one paragraph

Epic 4 was planned as eight stories about making fights legible. It became **thirty-seven cycles, of which two-thirds were not stories** — because partway through, the radar stopped being a feature and became the game's defining instrument. Stories 4-6, 4-7 and 4-8 were deliberately deferred behind a four-story radar physics arc (amendment 120), and that arc then generated eighteen correction cycles of its own. Every one of those eighteen began the same way: Eric sailed the shipped build, saw something wrong, and ruled. That loop is the most productive mechanism this project has, and it is also the reason the epic's plan bears little resemblance to its execution.

---

## Acceptance mode — and its one real gap

**There was no multiplayer playtest this epic.** Epic 3's headline acceptance was a ~10-player live session that ratified the ring's pacing. Epic 4's acceptance was Eric, solo, on the live site, in a tight correct-on-sight loop — which caught an enormous amount (the wake being "far as fuck", the terrain masked with the ship answer, grey NO-DATA, a hull rendering as non-uniform steel, the bounty copy grammar) and did so within hours each time.

The gap is specific, not general: **several Epic 4 claims are specifically about what a human eye picks out of a crowded scope under load**, and those have single-observer evidence.

- *"Camouflage is by structure, not strength"* (amendment 203) — a wake hides because it is the same pixel at the same opacity and the eye must pick a coherent LINE out of random dots. Measured as lit-fraction contrast; never tested against a second pair of eyes.
- `degradeAlphaFactor` (0.35) — whether a 4th-arriving muzzle flash at ~0.315 alpha for 120 ms is *catchable* under exactly the load that triggers it. Ships stamped as implementer draft.
- The readability gate's own headline claim, *"threat channels read first"* — **argued and unit-tested, not visually proven**, because it is a temporal claim and a still image cannot show motion-versus-hold. The gate says so in plain text rather than papering over it, which is the right behaviour and also leaves the claim open.

Epic 3's carried checkpoint (terminal-ring endgame, BB 50% share, island pockets) also went unaddressed for the same reason: no full-roster closure-reaching match happened.

## What Went Well

1. **The spec contract held for a fourth consecutive epic: 0 intent_gap, 0 bad_spec across 11 stories and 243 amendments** — through a mid-epic re-scope that inserted four unplanned stories, a wholesale reversal of Story 4.2's shipped grammar (amendment 62), and eighteen correction cycles. Two spec-rooted findings in 4-12 were patched in place rather than looped back, and that deviation was documented as a deviation rather than quietly taken.

2. **The cross-model review gate keeps paying, and Story 4-8 is its best single argument yet.** P1 — one flash budget fed from **two clock domains**, so a foghorn honk landing on the 64th claim deleted every region's onset history and the next three flashes animated when they should have degraded — was found **independently by all three models**, and violated the ratified 3/s floor at exactly the heavy-stack moment the floor exists for. Elsewhere the pattern is the opposite and equally valuable: 4-3's two models **overlapped on nothing**; 4-4's reviewers each found what the other missed; 4-9's Codex alone graded the dazzle divergence fix-first while Fable alone found the wire-resolution surplus.

3. **Correct-on-sight ran at full speed and was the epic's real design engine.** Eighteen radar cycles, most landing the same day Eric reported the problem. Several of those reports were *better than the spec*: *"if I'm looking at a side of an island and there's a mountain, my radar should pick up all the way to the peak on that side, right, and then the shadow lives on the other side?"* — a genuine model bug found by eye, whose fix left ship disclosure byte-identical while raising painted terrain from q182 to q251 against a true peak of q255.

4. **Deletion became a first-class ruling with a house style.** Grey NO-DATA removed end to end with **11 tests RETIRED rather than adapted** (cycle 69); the storm wall removed the same way at −194 lines and 11 more tests retired (cycle 72); the bounty suffix grammar deleted with its tests retired (amendment 222). The standing rule — *no dead knob survives a deletion ruling* — is now precedent three times over. Cycle 72 is the purest case: removed by **preference, not defect**, with nothing broken and no bound violated.

5. **The derivation doctrine reached its terminal form in the eighths ladder.** Intel range is the one ruler; every sensor boundary is a named eighth of it, SIGHT-anchored, with build-failing pins (`2RH = radarRange²/4` sits beside the ladder). Retuning truesight now moves gun range, star-shell flare, radar paint, muzzle-flash carry, smoke reach and the endgame ring together. Eric: *"That model is how I want to think about range from now on."*

6. **The perception boundary widened deliberately and never accidentally.** Six declared exceptions at epic open, six at epic close. The bounty ruling is the first time a proposed exception was **rejected outright** rather than accommodated: identity was already free via `syncRoster`, position was the only genuinely new disclosure, so deleting the bloom meant no seventh exception was needed. Every widening that did ship (`sp`, `hc`, `mz`, `sunk`, `sm`, `fh`) carries its own independently-reimplemented oracle.

7. **Performance got cheaper while the feature set grew.** Cycle 68's server radar gate is **0.55–0.74×** the cost of the segment test it replaced. Cycle 69 cut stored cells **−90%** on a coastal scope and roughly halved per-frame cost at every zoom, retiring cycle 68's own thin-headroom risk. 4-8's arbitration costs **~6 µs/tick against a 50 ms budget**, and degrading measured no more expensive than animating.

8. **The height raster bet paid off.** Shipped unread for nine cycles (Eric's 2026-08-06 ruling, on the explicit reasoning that retrofitting the storage later was the expensive path), then consumed whole by Story 4.11. This is the clearest instance yet of a deliberate architectural down-payment closing at a profit.

9. **The readability gate is the best evidence artifact the project has produced** — a 31-channel ledger with each channel's disposition, an adversarially-traced degrade table proving no channel loses presence/position/bearing, a staged scene where **nothing downstream of `bindRoom` is mocked**, a bundle-verification check that the dev-only staging is genuinely absent from production, and a plainly-stated account of what it does not prove.

## Challenges & Growth Areas

1. **The radar arc consumed the epic, and nothing predicted its size.** Four planned stories (4.9–4.12) generated **eighteen correction cycles**. Stories 4-6, 4-7 and 4-8 were pushed behind it. This was not waste — the radar is now the game's best feature and Eric said so directly (*"I am going to be completely removing all radar shit that existed before that. Its too good."*) — but the planning artifact never modelled that a "physical instrument" story arc iterates against a human eye rather than against a spec. **The lesson for Epic 5: whichever story is the one Eric will look at hardest is the one whose cycle count is unpredictable, and 5.4/5.5 are both visual-feel stories with named design-with-Eric gates.**

2. **High-severity findings returned: 12 across the story gates, against Epic 3's zero.** They cluster hard in the radar physics work — 4-10 alone had 5, 4-12 had 4 — and their shape is consistent: coefficients that were correct in the abstract and wrong against the shipped rendering path (surf painting BLUE on open water; the storm wall painting RED and out-reading a hull; the ribbon width being an exact class fingerprint; a one-frame weather collapse from a wrapped near-full arc). Epic 3's zero-high was earned on four stories with no numerical rendering model; this epic added one, and the finding rate followed.

3. **The noise-OFF fixture is the epic's defining test-quality lesson (amendment 135).** Every bound assertion ran a CLEAN fixture, so two coefficient defects survived a fully green 1,868-test suite. It is explicitly recorded as *"the cycles 54/55/57 shape again"* — i.e. this shape recurred at least three times before it was named. **A bound asserted against a fixture that does not include the shipped stochastic term proves nothing about the shipped path.**

4. **A ruling and its implementation bound have different authority, and the bound is what gets built (amendment 133).** Story 4-10's first implementation followed its spec's clutter bound faithfully and produced a haze that lit **zero pixels** — neither option Eric was shown. `bands[0].at` is a transparency threshold, so "peak strictly below it" makes clutter invisible rather than safe. Recorded lesson: *a numeric constraint is the spec author's claim and is reviewable; it does not inherit the ruling's authority.* Worth promoting from an amendment note to a standing review question.

5. **Rulings made before seeing the thing have a real error rate — three reversals of record this epic.** Amendment 180 (grey NO-DATA) was chosen by Eric from four options at a question gate *before seeing any of them on the water*, and **reversed by its own author on sight**. Amendment 110 (no torpedo wakes) was likewise reversed by its owner. Amendment 102's *"closer to a low island = longer shadow"* was found **backwards under its own formula**. All three were caught, but the pattern is clear: **question gates that ask about VISUALS have a materially higher error rate than ones that ask about RULES**, and the cheap mitigation is to prefer "ship it behind a flag and look" over "choose from four descriptions".

6. **A ledger entry can be wrong about what it is.** The 25%-vs-50% HP threshold was ledgered as documentation drift from Story 3-2 and carried an entire epic — and turned out to be a **category error, not a typo**: both documents were right and describing different things (crimson threat band vs amber warning band). Had it been "fixed" as drift at any point, the amber corollary would have become unimplementable and Tier 1 would have sat jammed on through the back half of every fight. **Ledger entries that assert a doc is wrong deserve a re-derivation before they are actioned, not just a correction.**

7. **Doc drift is now a three-epic backlog and it is growing faster than 7-5 can plausibly absorb.** Amendment 243 alone recorded **seven** new disagreements across DESIGN.md / EXPERIENCE.md / epics.md — the Listening Ring still reading as a ratified Tier-1 channel in all three spines despite amendment 1; UX-DR19 still carrying the Bounty Bloom that amendment 216 struck; the squint-test staging still saying "bounty active"; and two genuine *gaps* (no doc states what a Tier-2 channel does under another Tier 2, nor whether Tier 3 freezes under Tier 2 alone — cycle 75 implemented the ratified asymmetry literally and recorded that it did). The last two are not stale text; they are **unspecified behaviour that got an implementer's reading**.

8. **The deferred-work ledger is growing much faster than it drains: 178 entries at epic close, 8 explicitly resolved.** Story 4-7 alone deferred 11 findings at one gate. Individually every defer is justified and evidenced; collectively the ledger has stopped being a queue and become an archive, and no cycle since 2026-07-18 has run a triage pass over it.

9. **NFR1 whole-frame cost remains unverified at epic close.** The readability gate's first attempt produced 17 frames in 6 seconds beside a 1.1 ms frame time — *"two numbers that cannot both be true"* — because headless `rAF` is throttled, and Eric noted the Vite dev build was never a valid basis for an NFR1 verdict anyway. Targeted benchmarks were substituted and are excellent, but **no verdict against the 16.6 ms budget exists for the whole frame**, and Epic 4 added materially to the render path (return heatmap, shadows, wakes, chop, flash budget). This is now Epic 7's reference-device pass carrying a full epic of unmeasured render growth.

10. **Cycle 57 shipped a live regression.** World-locking broke and was reverted the same day (PR #108), with the version number reused by cycle 58. One incident in 37 cycles is a good rate; it is recorded here because Epic 3 had zero and because the fast-cycle cadence is what makes both the catch and the break possible.

## Key Insights

1. **"Realism is the idea source and the tiebreaker on presentation; fun wins on mechanics"** (amendment 115, superseding 100) is the epic's governing epistemology, and it was *earned by iteration rather than declared up front*. The wake is the clean proof: the physics said 12 s, Eric sailed it and cut it to 5.5 s — and the physics stayed in the constant as the recorded reason the number **can never be argued back up**. A physically honest number can be too much for this ocean.

2. **Deriving the replacement from an existing ruler beats picking a feel number.** The wake clock is not 5.5 s because it felt right; it is `detect / 45 u/s` — your wake reaches as far behind you as detect reaches around you — and it was written as a *literal* and pinned deliberately, so a kinematics retune cannot silently move a value Eric set. Same instinct as the eighths ladder, one level down.

3. **The absence is its own legend** (cycle 69). Grey NO-DATA was chosen to mark unknown water; seeing it, Eric took the option the acceptance criterion had called *"the thing your acceptance criterion calls a lie"* — because the island's far half is visibly missing and that IS the legend. Rendering an explicit marker for "nothing here" is usually redundant with the geometry that caused it.

4. **The right question for a ship is the wrong question for a mountainside.** Cycle 69's unification — `requiredHeight(D) = H · (1 − visRaw(D))`, with a ship as the own-height-equals-mast instance — is the epic's most elegant single correction, and it moved ship disclosure not one bit while fixing terrain entirely. **Terrain is a soft step at the ray; a ship is a column masked from the waterline up.** Reusing the ship's proportional fraction as a terrain intensity measures 0.02–0.15 on a real upper slope, i.e. would have looked like nothing changed.

5. **An upper-bound-only oracle cannot detect a channel getting SMALLER** (amendment 125). The independently-reimplemented `mz`/`sm` oracle asserted `dist <= SIGHT * 1.5` and kept passing **vacuously** once the halo moved to `SIGHT * 1.25`. Every perception oracle should be checked for whether it would fail if its channel shrank, not only if it grew. The related shape from 4-4 is the same family: a property-fuzz suite ran the new `sm` oracle **zero** times until a hull was seeded wounded.

6. **The wire may carry no finer resolution than the presentation actually consumes** (amendment 124) — promoted from a single review finding into a standing anti-cheat rule. If two buckets render identically, shipping both is pure disclosure. Its sibling from 4-12: **a channel that carries nothing must not cost wire** (amendment 211).

7. **Ordering bugs in accumulator code are silent and structural.** Cycle 68's QUERY-then-FOLD correction (advancing first folds a sample's own cell, so a tall island paints nothing) and cycle 69's entry-vs-sample-distance walk (which striped a continuously visible slope into 8 lit runs and 7 dark rows at the 14 u raster pitch) both produced plausible-looking output. Neither was a wrong formula; both were a right formula evaluated in the wrong order.

8. **Scope decisions that split a system by the QUESTION it answers survive review; ones that split it by convenience do not.** Amendment 241 is the model: tier arbitration stops at HUD chrome because *"hold at your lit keyframe" is coherent for a breathing widget and meaningless for a 120 ms muzzle flash at a world position*, while the flash budget covers everything because its ratified floor says "element **or screen region**". That reasoning is what kept 4-8 to one cycle.

## 🚨 Significant Discovery — Epic 5 needs one story re-derived before it is built

**Story 5.4 (Fog Banks) was specified against a radar that no longer exists.**

Its acceptance criterion reads: *"inside one, my truesight radius shrinks and I vanish from others' truesight — while **radar may still paint me**."* That sentence was written when radar was a binary LOS-gated blip. Epic 4 replaced it with a physical return model: one reflectivity × geometry-falloff curve, height-aware terrain shadows through a shared accumulator, server-rasterized hulls, wakes with their own clutter corridor, and a display-time near-range opacity mask.

Three things follow, and none of them are answered anywhere:

1. **The physical return model has no fog term**, and amendment 106's coefficient table is an explicit handwave that a new material would have to extend. Amendment 108 already ruled that *"fog and rain are their own epic-scale feature"* — that ruling is a direct warning about this story's size.
2. **"Radar may still paint me" is now a statement about a curve, not a boolean.** Does a fog bank attenuate the return, leave it untouched, or add clutter? Each is a different feature with a different anti-cheat surface, and the wake row (which paints *water*, not hulls) has to answer separately.
3. **Cycle 72 is the governing precedent for area returns**: a full outside-the-ring storm return was rejected because a 1/d² area material *"would own half the scope late-match and bury every contact in it."* A fog bank is an area material. Anyone specifying 5.4 must re-derive that question rather than assume an attenuation is the safe default.

**Recommendation:** treat 5.4's radar interaction as a design-with-Eric gate at spec time, sized against amendment 108, and expect it to be larger than its one AC bullet implies. **This does not block Epic 5** — 5.1, 5.2, 5.3 and 5.6 are unaffected — so the mitigation is ordering, not delay.

*Secondary, smaller:* **5.1's STEP_ORDER refactor is bigger than when it was written.** `world.step()` gained wake rasterization, radar-shadow-gated blip production and the per-segment wake gate during Epic 4. The refactor is not invalidated — it is more valuable now — but "pure refactor, tests green" is a larger claim against 4080 tests than against 2614. And **5.5 (Whirlpools) has an untested interaction**: wake is server-owned world state derived from pose, so a hull carried and rotated by a current should lay a curved wake for free — but torpedo wake under a current has never been exercised.

## Deferred-Work Ledger Sweep (standing agenda item)

**Ledger state: 178 entries at epic close, 8 explicitly resolved** (186 after this retro files its own seven). No triage pass has run since 2026-07-18. **New this epic, needing Eric specifically:**

- **`degradeAlphaFactor` (0.35) is an implementer draft awaiting Eric's eye** — it decides how much of a muzzle flash survives a 20-hull scrum. The readability-gate captures include a close-up of a degraded `muzzle` and `spark` expressly so this can be ruled on with evidence rather than in the abstract. Under the staged load, **92–99% of everything in the hot region is degraded** — so "degraded" is the worst case's *appearance*, not an edge state.
- **The foghorn's volume band can read quieter than truesight on a dedicated intel-truesight build** — contradicting Eric's original anchor (*"I always hear anyone within truesight range at full volume"*). **The obvious fix is wrong**: clamping inside `sightOf` would reintroduce a dazzle term into the band resolver, which is exactly what amendment 122 removed to make "dazzle cannot deafen" structural. A genuine design fork; needs a ruling, not a patch.
- **At 1.5× zoom the radar return cells overlap the top-center BR chrome bar** — `T+02:57` renders on a return blob. Not introduced by 4-8; it is the `return` grammar (cycles 62–72) meeting the Story 3.3 chrome bar. Fix shapes: a backing scrim, or excluding the bar's band from the return layer. Needs a design ruling.
- **The region flash budget is shared across channels and arrival-ordered, not importance-ordered.** One burst hitting five hulls yields five sparks + five hull flashes in one region; three animate, seven degrade, first arrivals win. That is the ruling working as written — but if playtest shows an important mark losing its ramp to an unimportant one, **the fix shape is a priority within the region, NOT a larger budget.**
