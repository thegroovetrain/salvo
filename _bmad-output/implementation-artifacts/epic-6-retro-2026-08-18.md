## The shape of this epic, in one paragraph

Epic 6 was planned as seven stories about making matches honest. It delivered six in four days, cancelled one outright, and spent **nine of its fifteen cycles on work that was not a story at all**. That ratio is the epic's real signature: the story work — a queue, a win-check seam, a bot brain, a solo door, a liveness route, a resume path — went in remarkably cleanly, six specs closing at zero review loops between them, while the *corrections* came almost entirely from Eric sailing the build. The single most important thing that happened is that **the project got its first AI opponents and immediately learned that its measurement instrument could not see whether they played well.** Three thousand bot-matches passed four of six quality bars and reported nothing about bots firing into cliffs for three unbroken minutes or chattering along the storm rim; Eric found both in one match, by eye, in minutes. Meanwhile the epic's four wire breaks all came from balance cycles nobody planned, and the four headline features cost zero wire between them — a sign that the seams built in Epics 1–5 are now load-bearing in the way they were designed to be. The epic ends with the game reachable, playable alone, and survivable across a refresh, and with the beta's remaining risk sitting almost entirely outside the code.

## What went well

**1. The 6-3 seam paid off exactly as promised — and it was Eric's override that built it.**
At the Story 6.3 gate the orchestrator recommended gap-close-and-mark-done, deferring the "AI combatants are participants" half to 6-4/6-5 on the reasonable grounds that designing a predicate with no consumer is designing blind. **Eric overruled it and built the full seam ahead of its consumer** (amendment 13). When 6-4 landed bots two days later, adding `'bot'` to `ShipRole` left **all three predicate bodies byte-identical** — roster row, personal hue, class silhouette, spawn-lattice slot, XP accrual, boon deck, kill feed and bounty eligibility all came free, with **zero client changes and zero schema movement** (amendment 26). This is the cleanest vindication of an owner override in the project's record, and it is recorded in the amendments as an override rather than as agreement, which is why it is legible now.

**2. The anti-cheat boundary went from true-in-fact to true-in-structure.**
Story 6.4's central claim is that a bot *structurally cannot* cheat. At the review gate three reviewers found it was true only by discipline: `BotSelfReader.get(id)` took an arbitrary id, the driver held a real `World`, `combat.js` re-exported past the lint ban, and — worst — **`observeSpectator`, the unfogged omniscient view, was exported from the very module `ai/` had to value-import**. A one-line edit was a total wallhack, catchable by no test or lint rule. The fix cost **one indirection**: per-tick `BotTickEntry` records carrying an observe thunk bound to the bot's own id, `ai/` holding zero `world.js` references, and each lint rule proven to bite before being restored. The general lesson is now recorded: **a capability boundary enforced by what the code currently does is not a boundary; the test is whether the next one-line edit could cross it.**

**3. Two review-gate findings that no amount of testing would have surfaced.**
Amendment 27 is the epic's best catch. The AC itself specified a ~250 ms staggered observe cadence and the gate priced it as harmless staleness. It was not staleness — blips and `tickEvents` live for exactly one tick, so a bot observing 1 tick in 5 caught ~20% of both streams — **and it resonated**: at 15 rpm a revolution is 80 ticks and 80 ≡ 0 (mod 5), so a bearing painting on a phase that was not the bot's slot was **permanently invisible to that bot**. Both boonable sweep rates alias identically. Two reviewers measured it independently with separate probes (30 paints vs 0). The ruling that came out of it is durable and general: **a fairness knob may slow a bot's REACTIONS; it may never delete its PERCEPTION.**

**4. The correct-on-sight loop is now the project's dominant quality instrument.**
Nine of fifteen cycles were interstitial, and four of those nine exist purely because Eric played the build (90, 98, 99, 101). The loop is fast enough that a defect found in an evening is corrected and merged the next morning, and it consistently finds things the harness cannot: the countdown re-rolling spawn positions (A19), bots firing through islands (A32), the population register colliding with the wordmark below 768 px (A43).

**5. PV discipline held under pressure, and the rule was written down.**
Both review passes on 6-4 argued for a PV bump because `CONFIG.bots` rides `WelcomeMsg.config`. The adjudication held at 40 on the governing precedent (amendment 24's `CONFIG.fleet`) and **verified the load-bearing fact directly — `welcome.config` has zero readers in `client/`**. The distinguishing rule is now stated so it is not re-derived a third time: *a CONFIG block bumps PV when the CLIENT READS IT, not merely because it rides the welcome.*

**6. Cancelling 6-2 was a good decision made on measured evidence.**
The roster-scaling machinery had been fully plumbed since the hex era and **had never once done anything** — `capRef == playerCap`, so the square root was always √1. The investigation measured rosters 2–20 across three seeds and found nothing technically blocking; what killed it was the *game* (at cap 2 the fixed 660 u endgame ring would be 56% of the water at 0:00, and Epic 4's entire sensor calibration is anchored to a 2800 u ocean). The dormant machinery was **deliberately kept, not deleted**, because teams would reopen the question in the opposite direction. That is a cancellation done properly.

## What was hard

**1. The harness measured that bots FUNCTION, not that they BEHAVE SENSIBLY — and this is the epic's central lesson.**
Story 6.4 shipped with 3000 bot-matches of evidence and six quality bars. Cycle 99 then found, from **one match Eric watched**, that bots had no line-of-fire test anywhere under `server/src/game/ai/` and had been firing into cliffs — reproduced at **three unbroken minutes, 72% of ordnance into rock** — and that the storm ring was never a constraint on a chosen heading, only a post-hoc override. Neither defect moved a single quality bar, because **a bot chattering across the storm ring still resolves matches and a bot firing into a cliff still spends its levels.** The tell that the LOS gate was an omission rather than a judgement call: the human already gets that readout (`aimPreview.ts` dims the burst circle) and `drones.ts` gates every fleet shot on live LOS — 6-4 ported that module's lead solve, island bias and un-beaching, **but not its LOS gate**.

**2. Two of six bot quality bars still FAIL, and they shipped that way.**
`Bots scoring ≥1 kill` measured 45.8% against a 60% bar; `storm deaths` measured 3.3% against a 5–20% band. Both fail on all three legs, so neither is a seed artifact. The diagnosis is credible — the first bar is structurally capped near 95% in a 20-bot match and is really measuring *profile balance*, and the second assumed matches would run long enough for the ring to bite when only 2.0% reach the endgame ring. **But the bars have not been re-derived or re-ruled, and they are the only quality contract the bots have.** Shipping against a failing bar with a written recommendation is the honest move; leaving it there across an epic boundary is the risk.

**3. Copy authorship kept overreaching, three times in one epic.**
Amendment 37 invented four states of SOLO-button copy from a ruling that said only *put a live count here*. Amendment 42 authored the queue modal's behaviour including an inert ESC. Amendment 34 chose a screen corner. All three were Eric-corrected within a day. The corrections cost little individually, but the pattern cost a whole interstitial cycle (101), and Eric named the underlying rule himself in A41. **A fourth instance is already sitting unratified**: amendment 49 flags `COULD NOT REJOIN YOUR MATCH — BACK IN PORT` as DRAFT COPY authored in-cycle because the AC demanded "a plain explanation".

**4. `spec-6-1`'s frontmatter drifted the same way `spec-5-6`'s did, one epic after it was fixed.**
Epic 5's retro fixed the instance and asked for a re-check. The re-check happened and passed for the *tracker pair*, but the spec file itself drifted again on the very next story. **Fixing an instance is not fixing a mechanism** — nothing in the pipeline asserts that a spec's own `status` agrees with the tracker's.

**5. The queue made solo playtesting impossible, and it was nearly shipped silently.**
Amendment 9 is a good catch recorded honestly: with a queue in front of the arena and `minHumans: 2`, one player could never start a match, and `npm run dev` did not set `HC_DEV_OPTIONS`, so **there was no way to reach an ocean solo from a browser at all**. That is Eric's primary playtest loop and it would have vanished without a line of warning. It was caught by the orchestrator rather than by Eric hitting the wall — but only just, and the story would otherwise have shipped it.

**6. The ledger is now three epics untriaged and growing faster than it is cleared** (220 → 250). It contains at least two items that are genuinely load-bearing for launch (below), and their visibility is falling as the file grows.

## Key insights

1. **A quality bar measures what it measures.** Six bars said the bots were fine; a human eye said they were shooting at rock. Both were true. The instrument was never wrong — it was answering "do these bots produce a resolved match with a sane kill distribution?", which is not the question "are these bots any good to play against?". **Every new evaluation harness should be asked, at design time, what class of defect it is structurally blind to.**
2. **The seams built in Epics 1–5 are now paying compound interest.** Four headline features — bots, solo mode, liveness, reconnection — landed at **zero wire cost** between them, and the epic's only wire breaks came from balance cycles retuning cards. That is what `isParticipant`, `effectiveStats()`, `frames.ts` and the signal registry were for.
3. **An override recorded as an override is worth more than one recorded as consensus.** Amendment 13 says plainly that Eric overruled the recommendation; amendment 26 could therefore say plainly that the override was vindicated. The project can now *learn* from that exchange, which it could not if the record had smoothed it.
4. **Perception boundaries erode by convenience, not by intent.** Nobody tried to give bots a wallhack; the omniscient view was simply exported from a module they legitimately needed. The fix was one indirection, and it was only found because a reviewer asked what the *next* edit could do.
5. **The correct-on-sight loop has quietly replaced the playtest checkpoint** — and it is not the same instrument. It is excellent at single-player defects and has found many. It is structurally incapable of finding anything that needs two humans, which is why the same item has now failed to close four epics running.

## 🚨 Significant Discovery — Epic 7 needs a plan change before it starts

**Three findings, one of which means Story 7.6 cannot pass its own acceptance criterion as written.**

**1. `loadTest.mjs` does not exist, and Story 7.6's release gate names it.**
AR12's load-test leg was deleted along with the drone fill in epic-5 amendment 41. Epic 5's retro action #5 asked 6-4 to rebuild the AR18 capability; 6-4 rebuilt the **bot-evaluation** leg only, correctly and well, and the load-test leg went unbuilt. Story 7.6's AC reads *"the pre-launch load test (the harness's second duty — `loadTest.mjs`) proves the deployed tier survives a portal-shaped connection spike, with `/metrics` confirming tick health under load."* **Verified at this retro: `server/scripts/` contains `batchSim.mjs` and thirteen smokes, and no `loadTest.mjs`.** Epic 7 must either scope its construction into 7.6 explicitly or re-derive the gate. This is the third epic this capability has been assumed present.

**2. An unauthenticated HTTP request can mint a full simulating room, and it is UNRULED.**
Story 6-5's queue-free solo door opened `ArenaRoom.static onAuth` to any request carrying `solo === true`. It is **safe against room hijack** — verified twice, independently, by a Fable pass and by Codex against `@colyseus/core` 0.17.44. What it opens is **cost**: a bare `POST /matchmake/create/arena` runs `generateMap` plus a 20-hull, 19-bot-mind, 20 Hz simulation for the ~15 s seat-reservation window, with no websocket and **no matchmake rate limit anywhere**. Bounded (no permanent leak), but a loop at ~10 req/s sustains ~150 concurrent full arenas on the single Render process. Before 6-5 no unauthenticated path could mint a simulating room at all. **This was accepted knowingly and ledgered rather than decided (`deferred-work.md:1108`), and it is a launch-readiness item that belongs in Story 7.6's hardening sweep.**

**3. Story 7.5's reconciliation list is now materially longer than its AC says.**
7.5's AC names EXPERIENCE.md's disconnect wording and the GDD's post-death "spectate" language. Epic 6 added at least four more: **UX-DR30's sailable weapons-safe waiting room no longer exists in production** (amendments 1 and 8 replaced it with a frozen held start line, and the grammar survives only for the dev/sandbox door); `EXPERIENCE.md:67`'s home sub-line copy was overridden by 6-5; `EXPERIENCE.md:108`'s "absence, not placeholders" was **scoped** rather than followed (amendment 39 — a population of zero renders); and amendment 41's copy law plus amendment 49's DRAFT string both want ratification in a copy pass. 7.5 should be re-scoped with these named, or it will discover them at the gate.

**Recommended action: hold a short Epic 7 planning review before create-story 7.1.** None of the three blocks Epic 7 from *starting*; all three change what its stories must contain.
