## Brainstorming Approach

**Selected Mode:** Guided-Freeform hybrid — the facilitator sequences the threads
(guided), but each thread is explored openly with techniques pulled in on demand
(freeform). Eric's ten-point equipment-rework direction is the session spine.

**Techniques Available:**

- Emergence Engineering — simple tag/slot rules → unexpected builds
- Economy Balancing Thought Experiments — regen/heal economy, per-charge cooldowns
- What If Scenarios — radical possibilities in the tag/slot space
- Failure State Design — work backwards from degenerate builds
- Constraint Box — slot legality as a creative limit
- MDA Framework — mechanics → dynamics → aesthetics checks per thread
- Player Fantasy Mining — the captain fantasy each fit expresses
- Core Loop Design — how the refit loop changes under one shared pool
- Moment Design — peak moments the new system should create

**Focus Areas:**

1. Positional slots — Q bow / E midship / R stern; slot identity as balance lever
2. Starting fits — one filled slot + slotless deck gun per hull
3. Equipment tags — [TORPEDO], [MINE], multi-tag items (Captive Mines)
4. Upgrade targeting — item-specific vs. tag-wide upgrades (Acoustic Homing)
5. Slot legality — bow-only Heavy Torpedoes vs. flexible Light Torpedoes
6. Universal Speed Boost on Shift (+20% top speed base)
7. Out-of-combat passive regen — 1% missing hull/sec, replacing level-up bonus
8. Menu heal as % of max hull — scales with tonnage
9. Per-charge cooldowns on multi-charge weapons
10. One shared pool — everything acquirable by everyone

## Ideas Generated

56 collaboratively developed ideas across six guided threads plus two that emerged
mid-session (the account metagame; the party-mode perspectives round). Eric's ten-point
direction was the spine; ideas below are what the exploration added, refined, or ruled.

### Thread A — Positional Slots & Loadout

**[Slots #1] Slot legality by weapon class** — Mines: stern or midship, start stern.
Light Torpedoes: bow or midship. Heavy Torpedoes: bow only.

**[Slots #2] Per-slot arc rules** — a weapon carries a different arc definition per legal
slot (Light Torps: forward tubes in bow, deck swivel mounts midship). The slot tells you
where it points; legality is geometry, not a compatibility table.

**[Slots #3] Tactical identity via placement** — the same item is a different weapon in a
different slot; build thinking includes *where*, not just *what*.

**[Slots #4] Mid-match inventory & swap** — players hold weapons and re-arrange slots;
moving a weapon to another legal slot to make room for a new acquisition is a real decision.

**[Slots #5] "Found" = drawn** — equipment enters play exclusively via the randomized
level-up draw. (Supply-drop and wreck-loot acquisition explicitly rejected.)

**[Slots #6] Tab becomes the loadout screen** — integrated with level-up offers, but
openable with zero banked levels for pure re-arrangement.

**[Slots #7] Single acquisition faucet** — one source (the draw) feeds equipment,
upgrades, and heals alike; no parallel acquisition systems.

**[Slots #8] Swap anytime, ~5s ready delay** — a slot that just received a weapon cannot
fire for ~5 seconds. Cheap anti-cheese against mid-duel hot-swapping.

**[Slots #9] Hand size: prototype both** — capped hand (forces discard decisions) vs.
uncapped; a discard/sell mechanic is desirable either way.

### Thread B — The Storefront Economy

**[Economy #10] Tiered card costs** — Tier I costs 1 level, Tier II costs 2, etc. Cost is
the balance knob for uniques (powerful ≠ rare; powerful = expensive).

**[Economy #11] Escalating heal price** — costs 1 once, then 2 twice, then 3 three
times... Heal-spam has diminishing economics instead of a hard cap; late-match heal
hoarding taxes itself (closes the sudden-death hoard-ceiling concern for free).

**[Economy #12] Heal decoupled from the offer** — taking a heal no longer burns or
rerolls the draw.

**[Economy #13] Paid reroll** — a separate reroll option costing 1 level.

**[Economy #14] Open the XP floodgates** — faster level rate to fund the richer economy.

**[Economy #15] Two upgrade scopes** — item-bound tiers (Light Torpedo's own ladder)
vs. tag-wide uniques (Acoustic Homing hits all [TORPEDO]).

**[Economy #16] Blind draw with soft affinity** — the pool never guarantees
build-relevant cards; a bounded weighting may nudge them. No hard build-filtering.

**[Economy #17] Tiered-equipment cards** — a drawn card IS the item at a tier (own none →
acquire at that tier; own lower → upgrade path). Acquire/upgrade collapse into one card
type; a blind draw is never dead. (Initially "worth testing"; became the preferred model
in the party round — see #44.)

**[Economy #18] Duplicate instances** — the same weapon in two legal slots (Light Torps
bow AND midship); tag upgrades multiply across instances; each instance has its own arcs
and charge cooldowns. An emergent build archetype nobody authored.

**[Economy #19] Storefront refresh** — buying refreshes all offer slots.

**[Economy #20] Card locking** (auto-chess style) — lock an offer slot to survive
refreshes; doubles as "reserve while saving up" for expensive cards.

**[Economy #21] Sell-back at cost − 1** — a 3-cost weapon refunds 2. Pivots are lossy
but never total losses.

### Thread C — The Tag Alphabet

**[Tags #22] Confirmed alphabet: [TORPEDO], [MINE], [GUN]** — [SENSOR] and [ENGINE]
provisional until a good use exists; [DEPLOYABLE] rejected.

**[Tags #23] Tags are pure upgrade affinity** — no slot rules, no draw logic, nothing
else hangs off them. The taxonomy's job is kept deliberately small.

**[Tags #24] Multi-tag items are deliberately rare** — Captive Mines [MINE][TORPEDO] is
the exemplar, not the pattern.

**[Tags #25] Radar buoy tabled** — removed from the pool. It shipped measured and the
bots learned it; it is simply a bad item that needs a redesign (future session).

**[Tags #26] Provisional [CANNON] tag** — star shells as cannon-type; Broadside possibly
[GUN][CANNON] (second multi-tag citizen if it sticks). Three overlapping upgrade
audiences from two tags.

**[Tags #27] Tagless items are legal** — no tag-wide upgrade ever touches them; a tag
earns its existence the day a cross-cutting upgrade wants it.

**[Tags #28] Equipment carries tags; upgrades target them** — Acoustic Homing wears no
tag; [TORPEDO] is its target selector. Two roles for the same word, never confused.

### Thread D — Class Identity & the Account Metagame

**[Identity #29] Class identity = hull + starting fit, nothing else** — same three slots
for everyone, same right to equip anything; identity lives in HP/kinematics/silhouette
and what you spawn holding.

**[Identity #30] Class-signature deck guns: parked** — interesting future; needs a
specific good idea to earn its place.

**[Identity #31] Starting fits (draft)** — TB: torpedoes bow; ML: mines stern; BS:
broadside or star shells (resolved as "both" via hull variants, #33). Noted: today's
shipped torpedo is, by real-world stats, a HEAVY torpedo — the split designs a *lighter*
fish, not a bigger one.

**[Meta #32] Account metagame** — persistent accounts, account XP, weapons unlocked into
your personal draw pool. "Everything in the pool for everyone" becomes "everything
you've unlocked is in your pool."

**[Meta #33] Unlockable hull variants** — same class, different starting fit
(broadside-start BS vs. star-shell-start BS as separate unlockables).

**[Identity #34] Light Torpedo identity** — slower fish (~45 kn real-world, slower than
the Heavy), less damage, wide firing arc, short reload. Heavy = the sniper's commitment
shot; Light = the forgiving, frequent, flexible-mount fish.

**[Meta #35] Unlocks gate variety, never power** — short total grind, unlock in any
order (pick what you want next).

**[Meta #36] Small-pool onboarding** — a fresh account's simple pool is a tutorial
disguised as progression. Ruled a happy accident for now; latch on later.

**[Meta #37] Hull variants are fit-only** (probably) — no stat variants; one hull to
balance, N spawn presets.

### Thread E — The Healing Economy

**[Healing #38] One combat-recency clock** — the XP-split window drops 60s → 30s, and
that same 30s defines out-of-combat. One definition, two systems, no drift.

**[Healing #39] Asymptotic regen is intended** — 1%-of-missing/s restores fighting shape
fast and plateaus near full; the paid heal is what *finishes*. Regen ≠ top-off.

### Thread F — Boost, Cooldowns, Scope

**[Boost #40] Speed boost: duration + cooldown, upgradeable** — [ENGINE]'s reason to
exist (+boost%, +duration, faster refill). The multiplicative +20% quietly preserves TB
identity with zero special-casing.

**[Cooldowns #41] Serial per-charge cooldowns — deliberate** — charges return one at a
time; the full dump has a real price. (Noted: current code has mostly 1-round pools, so
serial-vs-parallel has never truly been exercised — this is a choice, not a preservation.)

**[Scope #42] Wholesale catalog replacement, grafting expected** — new tag/tier/item
catalog from scratch; surviving machinery (cooldown scaling, stat paths, verb flags)
salvaged where it fits, not preserved by default.

### Party-Mode Perspectives Round

**[Ruling #43] Storm damage BREAKS out-of-combat regen** — Eric's ruling, story and
spreadsheet agreeing: the storm suppresses repair, matches stay terminal, the collapse
stays a collapse.

**[Economy #44] Tiered draw + replace-to-upgrade is now the preferred model** — the card
rolls at a tier; you replace your item to upgrade it. The item IS its tier: one number,
traveling with the weapon, no parallel ladder bookkeeping.

**[Economy #45] The merge mechanic** — two same-tier duplicates merge into the next tier
(TFT-style). A duplicate draw is never dead. Price curve falls out fair: Tier I + Tier I
(2 levels) = a Tier II (2 direct) — parity; premium grows with tier. Merge-rate-by-tier
is the tuning metric to watch.

**[Meta #46] The accounts deadline is an event, not a date** — "accounts ship before the
traffic push does." Before the push: no migration, no lost-unlocks support. After: every
week leaks retention.

**[Meta #47] localStorage unlock track as the cheap precursor** — no login, no email, no
GDPR delta; loses cross-device only. A candidate release-one metagame.

**[Architecture #48] Pool-as-filter from day one** — build the draw pool as a filter over
one universal catalog so account unlocks are content, not surgery, whenever they land.

**[Metric #49] Levels-wasted-per-match** — the storefront's gate metric (levels that died
in the bank or bought regret), plus merge-rate-by-tier; measured by the batch-sim harness
before any card is costed.

**[Bots #50] Bot swap policy is an unowned new verb** — the equipment-tactic seam gives
bots weapon knowledge, but *when to swap slots* has no owner. Must be designed with the
system, not after it.

**[Open #51] Cooldown-state-on-swap** — does charge cooldown state travel with the item,
reset, or transfer on swap? The real swap-cheese surface; unresolved, forge-time question.

**[Scope #52] The two-release trenchcoat** — release one: slots/tags/shop/regen (the
match game). Release two: accounts/unlocks. #48 is what keeps release two cheap.

**[Implementation #53] Per-slot arcs are the boss fight** — arcs keyed by (equipment,
slot) touches the wire contract, the aim preview, and the bot brain at once. The shop is
not the hard part; the arcs are.

**[Implementation #54] The Q/E/R migration is clean** — the keys already exist as
prime-slot inputs; "prime equipment N" → "prime bow/midship/stern" barely changes
`input.slot`'s shape.

**[Implementation #55] Storm-breaks-regen is cheap** — storm damage stamps the same
"hit" timestamp the combat clock already reads; no new state.

**[Open #56] Mine-laying vs. the OOC clock** — does laying a mine count as firing? The
Mine Layer's hang-back identity hangs on it. Tabled for the forge.

## Themes and Patterns

1. **The unification instinct** — one combat clock (XP-split = OOC), one acquisition
   faucet (the draw), slot legality as geometry, one pool, the item IS its tier. Eric
   consistently collapses two mechanisms into one; every collapse removed a future
   desync or bookkeeping surface.
2. **Levels become money** — tiered prices, storefront refresh, locks, sell-at-cost−1,
   escalating heal prices, merges. The refit window is becoming a shop, and draw luck
   becomes an economy the player can play.
3. **Identity without locks** — class = hull + starting fit; everything else is earned
   freedom. Unlocks gate variety, never power.
4. **Percentage scaling as silent class balance** — %-heal favors tonnage, %-boost
   favors speed; each class's edge is amplified by the same universal rule with zero
   special-casing.
5. **Fixes arriving for free** — escalating heal prices tax the sudden-death hoard;
   merge kills the dead-draw problem; replace-to-upgrade kills dual-ladder bookkeeping.
   (Murat's caution stands: when undesigned fixes arrive free, undesigned bugs are also
   in the mail — hence the metrics in #49.)

## Promising Combinations

- **Tiered draw + merge + lock + sell** (#17/#44 + #45 + #20 + #21) — together these
  make the blind shared pool playable: every draw is progress (merge), every save-up is
  safe (lock), every mistake is recoverable (sell). The four mechanics are one economy.
- **Per-slot arcs + duplicate instances** (#2 + #18) — the same weapon purchased twice
  becomes a coverage decision (bow narrow + midship wide), not a stat stack.
- **One combat clock + storm-breaks-regen** (#38 + #43) — disengage rhythm, XP crediting,
  and storm pressure all read from one timestamp.
- **Pool-as-filter + localStorage track + accounts-before-push** (#48 + #47 + #46) — a
  staged metagame path where each stage ships without reworking the last.

## Session Summary

### Most Promising Concepts

**Top Pick: The tiered-draw storefront economy (#10-21, #44-45)**
Levels become a currency with real prices; the draw rolls items *at a tier*; duplicates
merge upward; locks and sell-backs make luck playable. This is the piece that makes "one
blind pool for everyone" viable at all, and it generated its own fixes (self-taxing
heals, dead-draw immunity) during the session.

**Runner-up: Positional slots with per-slot arcs (#1-3, #18, #53)**
Bow/midship/stern as geometry, not inventory indices. The same weapon becomes different
tactical identities by placement, and duplicate instances become coverage decisions.
Flagged honestly as the implementation boss fight.

**Honorable Mention: The unified combat clock (#38, #43, #55)**
One 30s recency definition driving XP-split, out-of-combat regen, and storm pressure —
maximum system leverage for minimum machinery, and it closed the sudden-death regen
question by ruling rather than by patch.

### Key Insights

- The pool must be built as a filter over one universal catalog from day one (#48), or
  the account metagame becomes surgery instead of content.
- "Accounts ship before the traffic push" converts an anxious open question into an
  event-deadline (#46).
- Percentage-based universal abilities (heal, boost) silently preserve class identity
  without special cases.
- Two metrics gate the economy before any card is costed: levels-wasted-per-match and
  merge-rate-by-tier (#49).
- Bot swap policy is a new verb with no owner (#50) — design it with the system.

### Recommended Next Steps

1. Forge the storefront economy (tiers, merge, lock, sell, escalating heals) — the open
   questions list is its agenda.
2. Forge the slot/arc model (per-slot arcs, swap rules, cooldown-state-on-swap, the 5s
   ready delay) with the Q/E/R migration mapped.
3. Decide the release split (match-game rework vs. account layer) and the accounts
   mechanism (localStorage track vs. real accounts) against the traffic-push timeline.
