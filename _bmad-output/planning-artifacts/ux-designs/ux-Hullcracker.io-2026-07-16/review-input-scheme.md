## Overall verdict

The scheme is in far better shape than the one reviewed on 2026-07-16. The blocking finding of that pass is closed outright — CTRL is gone, `Tab` toggles, and the shipped chokepoint (`keyboard.ts:398-409`) is exactly the one-keydown, preventDefault-every-bound-key, modifier-chords-native contract the old 2.2 asked for. The 1–4 dual role the old 4.1 warned about is now the designed steady state, and the atomic-at-keydown rule it proposed shipped verbatim (`keyboard.ts:517-521`) and the spine states it (EXPERIENCE L149). **There is no critical finding and nothing here blocks the spine.**

What v3 leaves under-specified is not a *binding* problem but a *lifetime* problem: every new interaction — the belt digit, the held stream, the decoy prime, the countdown window, the armed rung — is stated at the moment it *starts* and is silent about what *ends* it. The three HIGH findings are all of that class, and all three are one paragraph plus a ruling each: **(4.1)** a digit mashed across the refit window's auto-close resolves as a card pick, then nothing, then a *consumable spend* — possibly of the card just stocked — because the shipped rule is atomic with no post-close grace, which was safe when the second meaning was nothing and is not safe now that it is an irreversible spend; **(4.5)** the machine-gun stream is a LEVEL (`InputMsg.held`, architecture D26) and the shipped combat lockout is a CLICK gate (`mouse.ts:102`), so a pointer already down when `Tab` opens the window keeps streaming under "full combat lockout" unless the spine says the level is gated — the same bug class the shipped rudder already guards against (`keyboard.ts:605-608`); **(4.6)** "firing auto-reverts to the gun" applied at press-down turns a six-second stream into a one-tick burst, and nothing says whether the revert happens at press or at release. Below those: eleven MEDIUMs, of which six are on the merged Ship & Deck screen (a DOM surface with ~200 focusable controls, a text input, and a press-then-confirm rail, given one line of key map tagged `[ASSUMPTION]`), and the rest are single missing sentences in the in-match rows.

## 1. Physical ergonomics

Reference posture unchanged: left hand on WASD (ring→A, middle→W/S, index→D), pinky on Shift, thumb on Space (bound-inert). The v3 additions are `Shift` (tap) and the top-row `1`–`4` with the window closed.

### Finding 1.1 — Belt digits pull the left hand off the rudder mid-turn — LOW (verified acceptable) `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L149 (`1`–`4` fire the belt), L154 (A/D hold), L311 (the belt maps spatially left→right).
`1` sits over Q, `4` over R: reaching `3`/`4` for a heal or shield lifts the index off D, and `1`/`2` lift the ring/middle off A/W. The rudder is a HELD key that self-centres on release (`keyboard.ts:464-468`, `605-608`), so a panic heal mid-turn briefly straightens the turn. This is strictly *less* reach than the retired CTRL+4 stretch (old 1.2) and the forge tested and rejected `Z`–`V` (extract-gdd-v3 §2.4), so the ruling is made. Logged, not a change: the belt is the panic row and it costs a tenth of a second of rudder — genre-normal (every ARPG belt has the same cost).

### Finding 1.2 — Shift tap under a held A — LOW (verified acceptable) `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L152, L166 ("the boost is a TAP, so the 2026-07-16 pinky-vs-A objection dissolves").
Pinky rolls onto Shift while ring holds A — the FPS sprint posture, and as a TAP the pinky is back in a frame. Verified; the objection the log closes is genuinely closed. One implementation note rides with it, at 2.4.

## 2. OS / browser collisions

The 2026-07-16 blocking section is closed; what remains is Shift-specific.

### Finding 2.1 — Shift+Tab is prevented-inert in the shipped chokepoint: rolling boost into refit silently drops the refit toggle — MEDIUM `[v3]`
**Location:** `keyboard.ts:392-397` (doc), `400-403` (`if (e.shiftKey && e.code === 'Tab') { preventDefault; return; }`); EXPERIENCE L148 (`Tab` toggles), L152 (Shift tap), L54 ("Tab still closes and reopens").
Shipped rule: Shift+Tab is the browser's reverse focus-cycle — prevented (focus must not escape the canvas) and **takes no action**. That was the right call when Shift was unbound. Under v3 Shift is a combat key the player taps under adrenaline, and the natural sequence *boost → open refit* is Shift-down, Tab-down, Shift-up (the tap overlaps the next key at real typing speeds). Result today: the boost fires on Shift's keydown edge, then Tab arrives with `shiftKey === true` and is swallowed — the window does not open, with no feedback, and the player presses Tab again. Not a browser hazard (it is prevented), but the spine does not say Shift+Tab is inert, and an implementer who "fixes" it by letting Shift+Tab toggle re-introduces the reverse-focus-cycle problem the exception exists for. **Needs a ruling:** whether Shift+Tab stays inert (state it in L166, so the settings reference and How-to-Play can carry it) or whether the chokepoint's Tab exception is narrowed. Note the symmetric case is already safe: Shift+`1`–`4` dispatch on `e.code` (`keyboard.ts:404`), so a still-held Shift never turns a belt digit into `!`.

### Finding 2.2 — Shift-modified pointer events: Firefox shows the native context menu on Shift+right-click despite suppression — LOW `[v3]`
**Location:** `mouse.ts:99-116` (button-0 only; `contextmenu` preventDefault on the canvas); EXPERIENCE L166.
Shift+left-click on the canvas has no browser default and `mouse.ts` reads no modifiers, so the boost tap never changes what a click does — fine. Shift+**right**-click is the one Firefox carve-out: Firefox opens its context menu on Shift+right-click even when the page cancels `contextmenu` (its `dom.event.contextmenu.shift_suppresses_event` default). Before v3 nobody held Shift in a fight; now a right-button slip during a boost tap pops a menu over the knife fight in Firefox, and the menu can eat the button-0 `pointerup` a held stream is waiting for (see 4.5). Known-uninterceptible; the spine's hygiene paragraph should list it beside Sticky Keys as a documented hazard. Chrome/Edge/Safari unaffected.

### Finding 2.3 — Shift during the refit window is an untagged design gap, tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` — MEDIUM, needs a ruling `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L86 ("`[ASSUMPTION: Shift is suspended with them; the lockout was ruled before Shift existed]`"), L146 ("Suspended while the refit window is open" — Q/E/R), L152 (no window clause); architecture D30 (Shift → `actSlot: 1` on the ability channel).
The spine is honest that this is unruled. What the reviewer can add: **the SHIPPED default is suspended** — the boost rides the ability channel through `slotAction`, whose first line is `if (isModalOpen()) return` (`keyboard.ts:492`), so with no new code the window swallows Shift exactly as it swallowed the Torpedo Boat's card-fitted boost. But the ruling that produced that lockout ("a captain picking a card is not conning") was about WEAPONS, and the window deliberately keeps the HELM live; the boost is now a universal *movement* verb sitting on the weapon side of the bar. Both readings are coherent: *suspended* (it is a slot with a cooldown, and the bar it lives on dims to 38 %) or *live* (it is helm, and the player who opens refit at full ahead should be able to boost out of trouble). The same question recurs at the held start line, where the answer is clearly "locked" (movement is locked there — `main.ts:2250-2252`). **Needs a ruling**; whichever way, the F row's model (L147: "suspended with Q/E/R") is the shape to state it in.

### Finding 2.4 — Two Shift codes; the keydown-edge semantic is implied, not stated — LOW `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L152 ("Shift (tap)"); `keyboard.ts:477-480` (`handleSlotKey` drops `e.repeat`), `531-541` (the ability FIFO queues presses up to `SLOT_COUNT`).
"Tap" is the right word but the spine should say the three things the implementer must not guess: the binding is on **both** `ShiftLeft` and `ShiftRight` (the chokepoint binds by `code`); the boost fires on the **keydown edge** and OS auto-repeat is dropped, so a HELD Shift is one press and keyup carries no meaning (no "hold to boost", no cancel-on-release); and a press while cooling **queues and rides the wire** (the server denies; `onAbility` predicts the pulse) rather than being dropped client-side, exactly as the old boost did. preventDefault on Shift's own keydown breaks nothing shipped — modifier state is still reported on the next key, and a focused text field goes native before the chokepoint sees it (`keyboard.ts:423`).

## 3. Mode completeness — per-surface key map

### Finding 3.1 — The countdown's key map is not stated; REDRAW has no key in a keyboard-driven moment — MEDIUM `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L54 (countdown row), L53 (boarding: "movement locked, weapons locked and radar off"), L108 (Denied: the held start line "is not a denied state"; v3: "a key on an EMPTY slot is denied"), L148; `main.ts:1022-1044` (`atStartLine`, `helmAxes` → dead axes), `2250-2252` (`combatLocked = modalOpen || atStartLine`), `1055-1060` (telegraph presses against a dead helm "accumulate unheard and are dropped whole… on the release edge"); architecture D22 L923-928.
The countdown is now the first *interactive* phase, and the spine describes its window and its button but not its keys. Derivable from shipped code, and it should be written down because two of the answers are non-obvious: **digits PICK** (window open → `handleDigitKey` → `handleRefitPick`, no start-line gate on that path — correct, the level-zero offer must be takeable); **W/S/A/D are inert AND their presses are discarded at the release edge**, never banked as an engine order (a captain who taps W×4 while reading cards does not lurch to full ahead at the gun — good, and worth stating since Journey A beat 2 has Marco "wiggle" the helm during this exact window); **Q/E/R, Shift, the belt digits with the window closed, and every canvas click are swallowed feedback-free** by the start-line lock (`slotAction` L493; `mouse.ts:102`); **F is live** by ruling; **ESC** closes the window (topmost) and a second ESC opens settings over the hold; **Enter and Space are bound-inert**. Two things need saying explicitly: (a) *precedence* — at 0:00 a belt slot is both EMPTY (denied by L108) and LOCKED (not denied, by the same row); the lock must win, or the countdown is the one moment a player mashing `1` gets red pulses for a slot that is empty by design — say "under the hold, the empty-slot denial is suppressed"; (b) **REDRAW is mouse-only** (DESIGN L310: "No other explanation"; the mock: "No ESC text") in a phase where the player's hands are on `1`–`4` and Tab. Enter is *inert in-match* today (`keyboard.ts:353-356`, `main.ts:956-962`) and Space is bound-inert — both are free — but giving REDRAW a key is a binding, so **needs a ruling**; if it stays mouse-only, say so, because the truthfulness rule (L89) then forbids a `⏎` chip on the button.

### Finding 3.2 — Mulligan idempotence is server-safe but the client's spend latch would turn a last-frame REDRAW into a denied pulse — MEDIUM `[v3]`
**Location:** architecture D22 L923-928 (`MULLIGAN_CHOICE` honoured iff `phase === 'countdown' ∧ !mulliganed ∧ offer !== null`, "anything else is a silent no-op"); `main.ts:751-760` (`trySpend` latches `spendInFlight`), `785-806` (a latch that neither acks nor moves the bank times out as `'failed'` → `pulseDenied(choice)`), `863-893` (`handleRefitPick`); EXPERIENCE L54, L128 (spend failure → denied pulse, level stays banked).
Server side the answer to "is a press in the last frame safely a no-op" is yes, by construction. Client side it is not yet: if REDRAW rides `trySpend` (the natural implementation — it is a `SpendMsg`), a redraw the server silently drops (phase already `live`, or already mulliganed) leaves a latch that never acks, **locks every card for the timeout** (`view.locked`), and then fires the 80 ms denied pulse — on choice `-2`, a card that does not exist, or worse on whatever `pulseDenied` does with a negative index. The ruling "silent no-op" must be carried into the spine as **the mulligan is not a latched spend and never produces a denied pulse or a lock**; the button simply vanishes. Also unstated and needed: REDRAW while a card spend is in flight (the view is locked — is the button?); REDRAW and a digit in the same frame (the spend consumes the only banked level, the offer becomes null, the mulligan finds nothing — a no-op, fine, but the *button* should not stay lit under a locked view).

### Finding 3.3 — Tab during the countdown takes REDRAW with the window; the auto-open must be an edge, not a per-frame update — LOW `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L54 ("Tab still closes and reopens"), L86, L148; `upgradeMenu.ts:1331-1339` (`toggle`), `1345-1354` (`update(view)` "never OPENS a closed window (only the TAB toggle does)"); `main.ts:854-858` (Tab with no offer → hide).
Closing the auto-opened window with Tab hides REDRAW (it is the window's child, not a separate surface) and reopening restores it — consistent, but say it, because a player who closes to look at the water and cannot find the button is the predictable case. Two implementation facts the spine should pin: the auto-open is an **edge** on the level-zero grant, because the shipped per-frame `update()` is deliberately pinned never to open a closed window (so a naive "if countdown and banked, show" re-opens the window every frame after the player closes it); and taking the card closes the window at zero banked exactly as it does live (`update(null)`), which also removes REDRAW — correct, since the offer is consumed and there is nothing to redraw, but the countdown row reads as if REDRAW persists for the whole 10 s.

### Finding 3.4 — Ship & Deck screen: the ✎ rename TEXT INPUT sits inside a layer whose own bindings are `1`–`3`, arrows, Enter and ESC — MEDIUM, needs a ruling `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L45 (keys `1–3` / arrows highlight `[ASSUMPTION]`, "ESC returns to home", CONFIRM SELECTION), L166 ("DOM overlay or text input with focus = keyboard suppressed from the SIM"); DESIGN L145/L304 (deck-column head: dropdown + ✎ rename + × delete), L305; `classSelect.ts:137-150` (`keyAction` on `e.key`; `Number(key)` → highlight; Enter → pick; ESC → close), L141 ("the caller suppresses this entirely while a text input is focused"), L696-705 (Enter/Space swallowed only while a *layer button* holds focus); `home.ts:1091-1095`.
The shipped class layer has exactly the guard needed (a focused text field suppresses the layer's window listener), but the spine's input-capture rule speaks only of the *sim*. On the merged screen the rename field is a text input inside the layer, so the rule must be extended to the layer's OWN bindings, explicitly: typing `3` into a deck name must not switch the hull to the Mine Layer; **Enter in the rename field** must commit the name and must not be CONFIRM SELECTION (which leaves the screen with the deck mid-rename and — see 3.6 — possibly unsaved); **ESC in the field** must cancel the rename, not return to home. Two further Enter homes now exist on one screen with no precedence stated: CONFIRM SELECTION (the shipped Enter ≡ CONFIRM), SAVE, and — if 3.5 follows the settings precedent — an armed rung's confirm. **Needs a ruling** on what Enter means on this screen outside a text field. (Also: with the dropdown MENU open, ESC should close the menu before it closes the screen — the shipped ESC stack rule applied one layer down; unstated.)

### Finding 3.5 — Press-then-confirm on the rail: what cancels an armed rung is not stated — MEDIUM, needs a ruling `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L112 (rung states; "amber + `⬢ n` = a hollow rung pressed, awaiting the confirming press"), L45; DESIGN L144, L303; register #50 ("press-then-confirm everywhere"); `settings.ts:523` and `main.ts:956-962` (the shipped precedent: an armed danger action confirms on "second click OR Enter" and never keeps focus).
The arming press is ruled; nothing says how it disarms. Cases an implementer must answer and currently cannot: pressing a **different** hollow rung (moves the arm? two armed? — if two can be armed and Enter confirms "the armed action" per the settings precedent, Enter is ambiguous and could spend two tokens); clicking anywhere else on the screen; ESC; switching the hull tile or the deck in the dropdown; leaving via CONFIRM SELECTION or ESC with a rung armed (silently disarmed, presumably); a timeout (the settings danger buttons have none — say whether the rail follows). The safe reading is "one armed rung at a time; any press elsewhere disarms" but that is a design choice with a cost (a player who arms, scrolls the grid, and returns must press twice again). **Needs a ruling.** Note the mock's own legend (`shipdeck-1.html` L235) lists exactly three presses — press, press again, unlock — and no cancel.

### Finding 3.6 — × delete and unsaved edits: confirm, floor, fallback, and what ESC / CONFIRM SELECTION do to a dirty deck — MEDIUM, needs a ruling `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L45 ("rename/delete", "the dropdown defaults to the account's last-used deck", "a deck is never a forced choice", "Edits after queuing apply to the next match; the client sends a deck id"), L112 ("press-then-confirm everywhere"); decision L42+82 ("a logged in user can delete or change [the starter]"); DESIGN L304 (SAVE unlit while illegal).
Delete is a one-glyph affordance next to a text field. Unstated: whether × follows "press-then-confirm everywhere" (register #50 was ruled for BUYING; a delete is the more destructive act and the spine should say it is covered rather than let it be inferred); whether the last deck for a hull can be deleted (the starter is deletable by ruling — so a hull can reach zero decks, at which point "the client sends a deck id" has no id to send and the class card's promise "a deck is never a forced choice" has nothing to fall back to); which deck becomes current after deleting the current one. And the dirty state: SAVE is a button, so an edited-but-unsaved column exists; ESC (home) and CONFIRM SELECTION (home, chip updated) with unsaved edits — discarded silently, blocked, or auto-saved? A captain who edits, presses CONFIRM, and sails the *old* deck will read that as a bug. **Needs a ruling** on all three; none is a binding.

### Finding 3.7 — SAVE "unlit" vs `disabled`; Tab order through ~200 grid controls; arrows scroll vs highlight — LOW `[v3]`
**Location:** DESIGN L304 ("SAVE… unlit (`.4` alpha, no glow) while illegal"), L284 (the retired rail's precedent: "goes genuinely `disabled` (keyboard and AT see it, not just the eye)"); EXPERIENCE L45 ("the collection grid… scrolls (the only thing on the screen that scrolls)"); `classSelect.ts:145-146` (arrows → highlight, `preventDefault` at L813); `keyboard.ts:10-11` (the chokepoint is post-join only — Tab is native on this screen).
Three small things. ## 4. Consistency & collisions within the scheme

### Finding 4.1 — The 1–4 mash across the window's auto-close: pick → (close) → *consumable spend*, possibly of the card just stocked — HIGH, grace needs a ruling `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L149 ("the digit has TWO meanings by the window's state, still read at keydown"), L148 ("It also closes on… spending the last banked level"), L86 (a pick latches; the window stays open; success renders the next offer in place "or auto-close at 0 levels"); `keyboard.ts:517-521` (atomic-at-keydown), `main.ts:863-893` (`handleRefitPick`: a locked view is inert), `785-821` (latch release → `update(null)` hides on a later frame); architecture D20 L873-875, D21 L860-863 (`stock` takes "the slot already holding that consumable, else the first empty consumable slot").
The old 4.1 asked for two things: atomic evaluation at the digit's own keydown (shipped) and a short grace after the window closes during which a digit resolves as *nothing* (not shipped — under refit-or-nothing it was unnecessary). v3 makes the second half load-bearing. Walk the designed sequence with one banked level: `Tab`, then `2` — the pick latches, the window stays open but locked, the server acks ~100-200 ms later, the bank reads zero, and the next render frame hides the window. A player who double-taps `2` (mash is routine; the shipped code calls the band's digits "the most-mashed key") lands press two inside the lock — inert, correct — and press three, at human cadence 200-300 ms, **after the hide: belt slot 2 fires.** If the card picked was a consumable, `stock` put it in the first empty belt slot, which for a fresh belt IS slot 1 or 2 — the third tap can burn the very card the first tap took. Both meanings are irreversible spends; the old finding's principle ("misfiring *nothing* beats misfiring the wrong thing") applies with more force now than when it was written. **Needs a ruling** on whether a digit inside a short window after an auto-close resolves as nothing; the spine should at minimum state the close-then-fire sequence as a known consequence if Eric keeps the rule as is. (Verified safe, for the record: Tab and a digit "in the same frame" is really event order — each keydown is dispatched synchronously and `toggle()` flips `visible` synchronously, so there is no frame-level race; and a digit HELD when the window auto-opens fires nothing until a fresh press, because `e.repeat` is dropped at L518.)

### Finding 4.2 — The belt digit must be stated as edge-gated against the ability FIFO — MEDIUM `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L149, L80 ("HULL REPAIR / SHIELD BLOCK / SMOKE SCREEN / CHAFF fire on their key"); architecture D21 L879-884 (instants ride `actSeq` + `actSlot ∈ CONSUMABLE_SLOTS`); `keyboard.ts:477-480` (`handleSlotKey` drops `e.repeat`), `531-541` (`activateAbility` queues up to `SLOT_COUNT` presses, drained one per 50 ms input), `551-556`.
Belt instants ride the same FIFO the boost does. That FIFO exists so several *distinct* presses in one 50 ms window each reach the server; it will just as happily queue OS auto-repeat of a held `1` at ~30 Hz — up to `SLOT_COUNT` (nine under v3) heals from one hold — unless the digit handler is edge-gated as the slot keys are. The shipped digit handler *is* (L518), but that is the refit path; the belt path is unbuilt and routes through a different entry. One sentence in L149: "one physical press = one consumable; OS auto-repeat never fires a second."

### Finding 4.3 — The decoy prime inherits the weapon prime rules, but the spine never says which — MEDIUM `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L80 ("DECOY BUOY primes on its key and fires on click into the mine's rear arc — the card face never says which"), L104 (Selected: "a belt digit on the DECOY BUOY"), L146 (weapon rule: "a weapon key primes its slot, the same key reverts to the gun, and firing auto-reverts"), L149; GDD L172 ("key primes, click fires… as a one-round pool on the same equipment interface"); `keyboard.ts:163-167` (`nextPrimedSlot`: same key → gun; any other weapon key → that slot), `684-686` (`revertToGun` on a predicted-fireable click only), `main.ts:3433-3439` (a predicted-DENIED click keeps the prime — "the mine's placement reach is part of its aim gate").
The GDD's "same equipment interface" makes every answer derivable, and every one should be written down because a belt slot does not *look* like a weapon slot: **same digit again = unprime** (revert to the gun, like Q/E/R — not "fire"); **Q/E/R after the prime replaces it** silently (the decoy is unprimed, no feedback, no charge spent); **a different belt digit while primed fires that instant AND keeps the decoy prime** (independent channels — `actSeq` vs `input.slot`); **a click outside the rear arc or past the leash is denied and keeps the prime** (the mine's shipped rule, `main.ts:3437-3438`); **a click inside fires and auto-reverts to the gun**. Also: the decoy's Selected state is the belt's ONLY amber state, so a stocked decoy pressed by a player expecting a heal-like instant goes amber and waits — that is the intended teach (§5.1), but the Denied row (L108) should say a prime is not a denied press.

### Finding 4.4 — Instant consumables have no invalid-state rule: SHIELD BLOCK twice REPLACES (a `[DRAFT]` that burns a card), HULL REPAIR at full HP is unaddressed — MEDIUM, needs a ruling `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L108 (Denied: "cooling, no ammo, empty slot"), L130 (HULL REPAIR), L224 (SHIELD BLOCK owner view), L81 (shield ring on the globe); architecture D28 L1079-1080 ("a second shield while one is up REPLACES it with a fresh 100 / 10 s — no stacking, `[DRAFT]`, ledgered"); DESIGN L284 (the retired rail REFUSED `AT FULL HP` / `SUNK` with a reason word and a denied pulse — `main.ts:876-889`).
The retired heal had an invalid-state rule (refuse at full HP, dual-coded); its card replacement has none in the spine, and the architecture's draft for the shield is the *most* expensive reading: a double-tap under fire spends two cards for one shield's worth of absorb, and the only tell that one was already up is a thinning ring on the globe nobody reads mid-fight. Three coherent readings exist — replace (draft), refuse-while-active with the denied pulse (the rail's precedent), stack — and the spine must carry one because the HUD grammar differs (refuse needs the belt slot to show a "not now" state the belt row L277 says it never has: "No cooldown, ever"). Same question for HULL REPAIR at full HP (spend into the regen pool — pure waste — or refuse as the rail did). **Needs a ruling** for both; whichever it is, L108's Denied trigger list gains or explicitly declines an "already active / at full" clause.

### Finding 4.5 — What ends the held stream: the shipped combat lockout is a CLICK gate and `held` is a LEVEL — HIGH `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L110 (Held fire: "release, arc exit or an empty pool stops the stream"), L158, L86 ("while it is open the game is under full combat lockout (mouse fire suppressed…)"); architecture D26 L1018-1026 (`InputMsg.held` "is a LEVEL: it rides `InputStore.latest`"; "Release, arc exit or an empty pool stops it; arc exit emits ONE `out-of-arc` denial at the transition, then silence"), L1032-1033 ("`mouse.ts` sets `held` = pointer down inside the primed stream's arc"); `mouse.ts:99-112` (the lockout drops *pointerdown* before the counter moves — `if (this.isLocked()) return`), `95-97` (`onBlur` clears only `inside`), `122-129` (listeners: pointermove / pointerdown / pointerout / blur — **no pointerup, no pointercancel**); `keyboard.ts:574-576` (`clearKeys` on blur — the stuck-key fix), `605-608` (`axes()` reads the rudder DEAD while an overlay is focused: "a rudder key held at the moment the overlay opened would otherwise keep steering… this is the invariant that holds even if a future open site forgets"); `main.ts:4347-4360` (`sendNeutralInput` on blur / `visibilitychange`).
The spine lists three enders and they are the *weapon's* enders. The *input's* enders are missing, and the shipped mechanism cannot supply them: the refit lockout works by refusing to count a click at pointerdown, which does nothing to a pointer that was already down when `Tab` opened the window — the level stays true, the sampler keeps sending `held`, and the stream continues under a window the spine calls "full combat lockout". This is precisely the bug the rudder already defends against by reading the level through the overlay predicate rather than trusting keydown suppression, and `held` needs the same invariant, stated: **the stream ends when the refit window opens (and at the held start line), and does not resume on close without a fresh press-down.** Likewise for **window blur / tab hidden / `pointercancel`** — no `pointerup` arrives after an alt-tab mid-hold, so absent an explicit clear the server streams until the pool empties (six seconds of fire from a captain who is looking at another window); the shipped blur neutral-send must carry `held: false`. And the listener that ends it on release must be on `window`, not the canvas, because a pointer that leaves the canvas over the kill feed and releases there fires its `pointerup` on the DOM element (`mouse.ts:124-125` already listens to the window for `pointerdown` for this reason). Firefox's Shift+right-click menu (2.2) can swallow that `pointerup` — one more reason the blur clear must exist. Bounded by the six-second pool, so not critical; HIGH because the spine's own sentence about the lockout is false as the mechanism stands.

### Finding 4.6 — "Firing auto-reverts to the gun" applied at press-down makes the stream a one-tick burst; the revert instant needs a ruling — HIGH `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L80 / L146 ("firing auto-reverts"), L110; architecture D26 ("if `input.held ∧ EQUIPMENT[primed].stream ∧ inArc ∧ n > 0`… the click counter is untouched"); `keyboard.ts:684-686` (`revertToGun` — "the special fires once, then the gun is the weapon again"), `main.ts:3433-3439` (`consumePrimeOnFire` runs on a NEW CLICK — `clickCount !== lastTickClick` — and reverts on a predicted-fireable click); decision L18+82 (the deck gun is SHOWN, keyless, at the head).
The shipped revert is keyed to the click counter. A stream does not touch the click counter (D26), so with no new rule the machine gun never reverts — acceptable — but an implementer following "firing auto-reverts" literally, at the first shell, produces this: tick 1 the primed slot is the machine gun and one shell fires; the prime reverts to slot 0; tick 2 `EQUIPMENT[primed].stream` is false and the level is ignored (pinned). The player holds the button and gets one round. The spine must say **when** the machine gun's prime reverts — at *release* (or pool-empty / arc-exit), never at press — and it should say what revert means now that the gun has a slot: revert = slot 0, the keyless deck-gun square at the head of the row goes Selected. (The brief's question "what does revert mean when the gun is off the bar" is answered by ruling — it is on the bar, decision L18+82.) Also unstated: Q/E/R pressed mid-stream switches the prime, the stream stops with no denial (it is not an arc exit) — say it. **Needs a ruling** on the revert instant; the rest is one sentence.

### Finding 4.7 — `held` with a non-stream prime: the client must draw nothing — LOW `[v3]`
**Location:** architecture D26 ("`held` with a non-stream slot primed is ignored, pinned"); EXPERIENCE L110 (Held fire is "MACHINE GUN click-and-HOLD"); DESIGN L276 (held-fire = amber outline + floor drain).
The server side is pinned. The client side needs its mirror: the held-fire slot grammar renders only when the primed slot is a stream kind AND the pool is draining — never from the pointer level alone — and a pointer held on a non-stream prime is an ordinary click (the counter increments once at pointerdown; nothing else happens). Otherwise a mine-primed player who holds the button sees an amber drain on a slot that is not firing.

### Finding 4.8 — A greyed card's digit: the shipped spend latch would produce the forbidden denied pulse and a 1.5 s lock unless the client sends nothing — MEDIUM `[v3]`
**Location:** EXPERIENCE L86 ("GREYED before the press… the server silently refuses it, no denied pulse; the card stays in the offer"), L108 ("a GREYED refit card is NOT a denied state: the server silently refuses and nothing pulses"), L128; architecture D21 L885-889 ("a refused pick is a silent no-op on the server… the client greys the card with the same predicate. No `denied` view is added"); `main.ts:863-893` (`handleRefitPick` → `trySpend` for any `choice < options.length`), `751-760`, `785-806` (no ack, no bank movement → `'failed'` → `pulseDenied(choice)`; the view is `locked` meanwhile).
Both spines say "silent". The shipped client makes silence impossible by accident: a digit on a greyed card is still `choice < options.length`, `trySpend` sends it, the server no-ops, the latch never acks, every card locks for the timeout, and the latch times out as `'failed'` — firing the exact 80 ms denied pulse on the exact card the ruling says must not pulse. The rule that makes the ruling true is a client rule and belongs in the spine: **a greyed card's digit (or click) is a no-op that sends nothing** — no latch, no lock, no pulse. (And, since "denied input is never silence" is a law at L238: the spine's reconciliation "it is never pressable" holds only if the press is genuinely inert, which is what this sentence buys.)
