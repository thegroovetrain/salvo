## 1. Issue Summary

**Problem statement.** Hullcracker began as a Battleship clone whose pillar is *"shoot around in the dark."* Today's game has that inverted: radar (1,500 u) out-sees the guns (1,000–1,250 u) and both are tiny against a 10,000 u board — on a ship scale where 1,500 u is 3.3 km and a real battleship's guns and search radar both reached ~40 km. Eric's Friday sitting (2026-11-07) asked for realistic reach, *compressed but not to today's degree*, cheaply in performance terms, trusting the ring, the beacons and the islands to force proximity. The forge hardened it to twelve locks and one restated pillar:

> **"A marine radar simulator that happens to have a naval battle royale attached."** The set works like a real marine radar; realism of the set decides every knob question; the game's goal is fun; **ratios are what is kept, not kilometers.**

The twelve locks (forge, 2026-11-07), each Eric's:

1. **Clear-day dark is RESOLUTION, not range.** Radar and guns reach about equally far (real: Iowa 38.7 km / Yamato 42 / Bismarck 36.5 / Warspite 30; SG set ~40 km). A contact is an **ARC**: sharp in range, wide in bearing, wider than a salvo pattern at gun reach. **No fire-control set** (a written-down departure from realism); splashes are what sharpen the arc. Supersedes Friday's "radar 35 / guns 25–30" spread.
2. **No AIM row.** Five index rows — ARMOR, POWER, RELOAD, ENGINES, INTEL. Arm (b) of #79 IS the design, not an experiment. If clear-day gunnery is too random the dial is the dispersion curve in `CONFIG`, never a row. The maxed-hull random-row rule (amendment 13) rolls five.
3. **Dispersion**: an ellipse growing linearly with range — ~1 % of range along the shot, ~1/3 of that across `[dial]`; a small floor at point blank; flat damage anywhere in the pattern; the same function for the secondaries; a pure shared function seeded from the match stream; the two percentages are `CONFIG` dials. Point-blank is a no-miss knife fight, accepted. Error ranking at gun reach: arc > lead over ~20 s of flight > pattern.
4. **Time compression is explicit.** One factor over real time on every RATE (hull speed, shell flight, reload, sink); "knots" is a label. The clock stays 16 min.
5. **The board STAYS 10,000 u.** The "big ocean" is real REACH on today's ocean: one scale factor (~0.5, a real 44 km board onto ours) maps every real distance; reaches, optics and torpedo runs are set by **real ratio to the board**, never by km. Targets `[dial]`: radar ~9,000 u (from 1,500), guns ~8,700 u (from 1,000–1,250), hull visible ~4,600 u (from 500), class-ID by eye ~3,000 u. Hulls stay real size. Ring ladder, center-shift cap and reveal beat stay (today's tuning sits at the playable limit). Map gen, raster, islands untouched. Friday's 40–50 km / 18,000–23,000 u numbers are superseded.
6. **Beacon placement**: a beacon is charted at the **midpoint between two neighboring hulls** (cycle 0 = adjacent spawn pairs; later cycles pair survivors, nearest unpaired neighbor), nudged toward the ring center until inside the hypothetical next ring by at most one TIME dial (30 s of baseline sailing `[dial]`). Keeps min spacing and the captain keep-out disc. Replaces the fleet-anchor max-min rule (which pushed beacons AWAY from hulls). Counts 8/4/2 stand; the "10k band" is retired.
7. **Gain = server-made false returns.** Above a gain threshold the server adds seeded **ghost** returns to that observer's frame, indistinguishable from a one-sweep real return; the rate rises with gain and weather; a ghost never repeats in place, a ship does (persistence is the skill, phosphor memory the tool); ghosts must sometimes land near real ships. The client draws nothing it wasn't sent. EVERY knob that shapes the paint is a server-side input through `inputs.ts` (PV bump).
8. **The set**: a modern chart-overlay display (what the client already paints) with a WWII ANTENNA — one fixed wide beam (~5–6° `[dial]`), 15 rpm base (INTEL tunes it). Player knobs, all real marine controls: **gain, sea clutter, rain clutter, pulse length, range scale.** Beam width and sector scan are NOT player knobs (Friday's #15 re-ruled); **beam width is a `CONFIG` dial.**
9. **Splash = a one-sweep radar return** (a short-lived scatterer the size of the water column; same arc fuzz; no owner; through `observe` with its own perception-invariant line — NOT a seventh exception). **Shells in flight stay sight-only.** The miss is information for both sides.
10. **Weather = a return-STRENGTH model** (falls with range, target size and rain on the path; painted iff it clears the gain threshold; ghost rate rises with gain and local clutter). Two axes, light × precipitation. **First build = RAIN (day) only**: the fog field IS the rain — sight cut in bands as today + weakened real returns + raised ghosts; the rain-clutter knob trades the band for weak returns. Clear, night/clear and night/rain are TEST ARMS after the primary works. Night optics ≈ today's 500 u sight. The server rolls once at room creation, on the welcome beside `fogSeed`, nothing on the schema; unknown at queue (#100 ruled).
11. **Min zoom = the whole board.** Markers hold screen size. Phone is not a target.
12. **The pillar statement is itself a lock.**

**Killed (forge):** MARPA (a drawn course / speed vector — erodes "lead in your head"; out, like fire control); the AIM row; beam-width and sector-scan knobs; a km-fixed beacon band; the fleet-anchor beacon rule; true knots on a 10 km board; true knots in an hour-long match; the 44 km board as such.

**What it collides with.** Epic 10 as re-cut 2026-11-04 is untouched in content: 10.9–10.14 land on today's ocean (Eric's ruling 3 below). The Ledger, the Front Door and the Account keep their text and shift one number right. **Issue type.** Strategic re-plan from the stakeholder: a new design of reach, sensing and weather, discovered in a design sitting and hardened in a forge. Not a technical limitation (the code runs as built), not a failed approach.
